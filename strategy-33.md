# STRATEGY GUIDE — Scenario 33: “The Two Petitions”

*Game documentation for “The First 90 Days,” a fictional simulation. This guide describes play in this scenario only. It is general information about how the simulation works — not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Ending — Goal Change Denied, Exception Documented**

State it honestly: this is a **partial** win, and the scenario does not pretend otherwise. The goal change is denied or deferred and an ASFA exception is documented in the record. Lark does not come home in this ending — no ending in this scenario returns her. The case continues, the plan's remaining work remains, and what you have won is the record position the rest of the case will be played from: a current Track A file, and a documented answer to the fifteen-month clock. That is the best ending this scenario offers, and it is genuinely the best — the alternatives are the same changed goal with less and less of your record surviving it.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

- **The fifteen-month clock has documented exceptions.** When a child has been in foster care for 15 of the most recent 22 months, the agency is pressed to file a petition to terminate parental rights — unless an exception applies: the child is being cared for by a relative; a compelling reason that filing would not be in the child's best interest is documented in the case plan; or the agency has not provided the services the plan identified as necessary for the child's safe return (42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E)). The route wins by making one of those exceptions *documented*, not merely true — and in this scenario the services exception is provable from the agency's own service-plan page.
- **Your own record is yours on request.** The case record — including the full assessment, its form, its item ratings, and its outcome — “shall be released upon request” (55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)). Everything the route discovers, it discovers because the parent read the file before the hearing did. (The guide notes, as the game does, that whether the adjudication-phase disclosure rule, Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(B)(1), reaches every scored item of an assessment form is untested; the route's request does not depend on that question.)
- **Counsel is a right, not a favor.** The parent has the right to counsel, and to appointed counsel if they cannot afford one (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337). Every structural move on the route — the timelines, the letter, the lead argument — is counsel's craft applied to the parent's paper.
- **A goal change is not a termination.** Had any later petition ever been filed, the agency would still have had to prove its grounds by clear and convincing evidence. Keeping the exception documented is what keeps that day from being scheduled on an incomplete record.

## (4) The traps

- **Skipping the record request** at *The File, Including the Form* (“one fight at a time”). Costs you the referral page, the assessment pages, and any services exception: you cannot document a gap you never saw. Every strong ending in this scenario is downstream of this stamp.
- **Raising the owed referral by phone** at *Chasing the Referral, on Paper*. Costs you the dated ask and the dated answer; by September the conversation is two memories, and the file records neither.
- **“Keep your head down”** at *Choosing the Lead Argument*. Costs you the documented exception itself: “progressing” is a summary word, and September is conducted in documents. This choice routes directly to the thin hearing, where the goal is changed.
- **Stopping attendance after the goal changes** at *The Fifteen-Month Hearing — Changed*. The most understandable trap in the scenario, and the most expensive: it converts a changed goal into **Ending — The Quiet Collapse**, because the file has a column for attendance and no column for grief.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The route controls the record. It does not control the judge, who could weigh Lark's settled year with the foster family more heavily on exactly this paper; it does not control the workers, whose unchecked referral box was an accident of an ordinary office and whose honesty about dates, in the winning hearing, was a choice they made; and it does not control luck — a referral sent on time would have deleted the route's strongest document, and a different foster family could have rewritten every visit scene. Outcomes in this scenario turn on workers, judges, and luck as well as on the parent. What strategy changes is which facts the calendar meets when it arrives — two columns, current, and impossible to summarize honestly using only one of them.
