# Strategy Guide — Scenario 108: "The Renewal That Went to the Daycare"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "Ending — Attributed, On the Record."**

State it honestly: this is a **partial** win, and it is the best this scenario offers. At the review on Tuesday, July 7, 2026, the two missed sessions are attributed on the record to the paperwork gap — a renewal form mailed to the daycare, whose printed return date (Friday, May 29, 2026) passed in the daycare's mail — the tally is corrected to carry its cause, the renewal stands filed, and the class is extended by two sessions so the missed ones can be made up. Kendra does **not** finish anything at this ending: the make-up sessions are ahead of her, the case is still open, and Cato — home throughout, as he was from the first page — remains home under supervision. The win is that the gap stops being recorded as a fact about the mother. Anyone selling you a bigger one is selling you a different game.

## (2) The route

Verified by walking the scenario graph from the start node (see the verification note at the end). Key decision points, in order — node title, winning choice, and what it changes:

1. **Turned Away at Drop-Off** — *Call the childcare office from outside the daycare, with the signed form in your bag.* Hidden item: the first missed session enters the file's quiet record together with its cause — the form had been mailed to the daycare. The call happens on the lapse day itself, Tuesday, June 2, 2026, before the file's one-word version ("missed") can become the only version there is.
2. **It Went to the Daycare** — *File the renewal with a dated written statement of the facts, keep copies of everything, and ask the office to confirm receipt in writing.* Cash −5. Sets **renewalFiled**; hidden item: the filed statement itself. This is the scenario's foundation stone: the renewal stops travelling alone and starts travelling with its own explanation, dated while the gap was still new.
3. **Filed, With Copies** — *Keep the confirmation with the statement.* Hidden item: the office's written confirmation of receipt. The office has now documented the filing in its own writing — the strongest kind of document there is, because its author is the system.
4. **The Second Tuesday** — *Ask the daycare in writing to confirm exactly which dates the lapse covered.* Sets **lapseConfirmed**; hidden item: the written ask. Tuesday, June 9, 2026 is the day the tally reaches two; it is also the last day the gap's exact shape can be fixed by the desk that watched it, before memory and staff rotas start their ordinary work.
5. **The Dates, in Writing** — *Keep the daycare's letter with the office confirmation.* Hidden item: the daycare's written confirmation — the lapse covered exactly Tuesday, June 2, 2026 and Tuesday, June 9, 2026, Cato was turned away at drop-off on both, and the renewal form had arrived in the daycare's own mail. A document authored by neither side of the argument, describing its own entryway.
6. **Ask for the File, or Don't** — *Put the request in writing — sign-in sheets and attendance notes — and keep a copy.* Cash −3. Sets **recordsRequested**. The packet that arrives holds the pairing the whole scenario turns on: the class sign-in sheet, signed on every Tuesday except the two lapse Tuesdays, sitting beside the progress note's tally, "missed sessions: 2," with no cause recorded.
7. **The Sign-In Sheet** — *Keep the packet with the daycare's letter and the office confirmation.* Hidden item: the sign-in sheet's evidence, preserved in date order. The system's own sheet now answers the system's own note, and both are in the same folder as the desk's letter.
8. **One Page, or a Story** — *Build the one-page timeline from the statement, the letters, and the sign-in sheet, in date order.* Sets **timelineBuilt**; hidden item: the assembled timeline. Seven dated lines — the form's printed date Friday, May 29, 2026; the form found Monday, June 1, 2026; the lapse and first missed class Tuesday, June 2, 2026; the renewal filed Saturday, June 6, 2026; the second missed class Tuesday, June 9, 2026; the daycare's confirmation Thursday, June 11, 2026; the sign-in sheet obtained Monday, June 22, 2026 — and no adjectives for anyone to argue with.
9. **The Review** — *Present the one-page timeline — the dated statement, the daycare's written confirmation, and the sign-in sheet attached.* This choice is flag-gated: it is only available with **renewalFiled** + **lapseConfirmed** + **recordsRequested** + **timelineBuilt** all set. It leads to **Ending — Attributed, On the Record**. (The review trip itself costs $6 of fare at **The Night Before the Review**; you finish the route with $136 of the $150 start.)

Supporting route texture: **The Flyer in the Folder** is where the record right is learned — it is taught by a waiting-room flyer, because in real cases it very often is — and **Missed Sessions** is where the arrangement's return (Cato covered again, the class attended and signed for on Tuesday, June 30, 2026) is set deliberately beside the tally that survives it, because in real files the tally very often does.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **Agency records are obtainable on request** — 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d) ("shall be released upon request"). Step 6 is that right, exercised in writing. It matters because the sign-in sheet's two blank lines — signed every Tuesday, blank on exactly the lapse Tuesdays — are the strongest evidence in the scenario, and you cannot quote a sheet you have never been shown.
- **Counsel presents the record in the room where the plan is measured against it** — the right to counsel, and to appointed counsel if a parent cannot afford one (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337). The lawyer's work here is not argument in the dramatic sense; it is sequence — asking that the record be completed before the "missed sessions" line is weighed.
- **No childcare-subsidy rule is stated, in the scenario or in this guide, because none is in the verified set and none is needed.** The renewal form and the office's letters state their own instructions on their faces, inside the fiction; the story turns on where the form was mailed, what the desks confirmed in writing, and what the sign-in sheet showed — not on any program's rulebook.
- Everything else on the route is generic process, deliberately without citation: file with a **dated statement** so the filing carries its own explanation; get the lapse's dates **confirmed in writing by the provider who watched them**; assemble dates in order. The scenario's thesis is that reviews weigh the record that exists when they sit — and that a parent can, lawfully and cheaply, change what that record contains about a gap she did not cause.

## (4) The traps

1. **Posting the form alone at "It Went to the Daycare"** and letting the office's note of your call carry the explanation. Cost: no **renewalFiled** — no statement, no copies, no confirmation in your folder. The review's gated presentation closes, and the route ends at **Ending — Missed, As Tallied**: the renewal lands, Tuesdays are covered again, and the two misses stay in the file unexplained.
2. **Taking the desk's word at "The Second Tuesday"** because they were there and they know. Cost: no **lapseConfirmed**. The desk's knowledge is real and entirely unattachable; by review day it is a memory of an entryway conversation, and the ending is again **Ending — Missed, As Tallied**.
3. **Waiting for the review at "Ask for the File, or Don't"** to explain the mail tray in person. Cost: no **recordsRequested**, so the sign-in sheet's blanks are never seen, never quoted, and never set beside the tally. A spoken account is believed personally and set aside professionally; the ending is **Ending — Missed, As Tallied**.
4. **Going in with the story at "One Page, or a Story"** because the story is clear, in order, and true. Cost: no **timelineBuilt**. The tally in the file — two words and a number, printed, already read — outlasts the telling, and the ending is **Ending — Missed, As Tallied**.
5. **Presenting partial paper at "The Review."** Cost: the whole route's assembly work. Half a timeline is an argument with missing premises; the gated choice exists precisely because the room can only complete a record that arrives complete.

## (5) What no strategy controls

Whether the daycare kept the form or threw it away — this daycare kept it, and a scenario whose daycare discards its mail has no confirmation beat for anyone to play. The daycare's staffing, and whether the desk that watched the lapse is still the desk that would be asked about it in a month. How long the office took to land the renewal once filed: the second Tuesday was lost inside that processing, on the winning route too, and no paperwork of the parent's shortens it. The reviewer's temperament: a different one could note the timeline and keep the tally's wording anyway, because Cato's Tuesdays are real too, and he spent two of them two years old in an entryway while a form travelled. The route controls none of that. It controls only whether, when the uncontrollable is weighed, the two Tuesdays arrive in the room as a documented paperwork gap or as an unexplained tally with a mother's name beside it. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, desks, and luck as much as on any parent's choices; the strategy's claim is the narrower one the scenario is named for — when the system's form goes to the wrong building, make the system's own desks sign for where it went.
