# Strategy Guide — Scenario 72: "The Word 'Minimal'"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "The Record Says Otherwise."**

State it honestly: this is a win at a permanency review, and a review was never going to do more than this in one morning. The court finds substantial compliance (§ 6351(f)(2)) and substantial progress toward alleviating the original circumstances (§ 6351(f)(3)); the goal stays reunification; unsupervised day visits are ordered as the named next step, with the next review date set from the bench. Ravi does **not** come home at the end of this ending — placement continues while the ordered step runs. The win is that the word in the order is "substantial," not "minimal," and that the step the case takes next is written in an order instead of hoped for in a hallway.

The **partial** ending is **"'Minimal' Becomes 'Moderate'"**: the findings improve — compliance "moderate," progress noted — but placement continues unchanged and no step is ordered, so the next review carries the fight. It is labeled partial because that is exactly what it is.

## (2) The route

Verified by walking the scenario graph from the start node (see the verification note at the end). Key decision points, in order — node title, winning choice, and what it changes:

1. **The File You Have Never Seen** — *Sign and send the written request for the agency record, keeping a dated copy.* Sets **recordsRequested**; hidden item (received at **Mail Day**): the visit logs released on written request under § 3130.44(d), reading "engaged, prepared" across 24 visits. This is Hinge A: without the agency's own logs, the review is Deanna's word against the report's adjective.
2. **Copies, Tabs, an Index** — *Copy and organize the packet — certificates and attendance, tabbed and indexed.* Cash −15 (you finish the route with $95 of the $110 start). Sets **exhibitsReady**. This is Hinge B: evidence reaches a judge by being offered — handed up, marked, admitted — and judges do not take tote bags into evidence.
3. **The Letter Problem** — *Have counsel arrange the provider's attendance records — dates and stamps, not adjectives.* Sets **providerProof**; hidden item: the therapy attendance record (22 of 24) and parenting certificate, marked and admitted. This is Hinge C: a therapist's warm letter cannot be cross-examined — and cannot be believed the way a dated record can.
4. **The Error in the Appendix** — *File the written rebuttal through counsel, correcting the attendance error with the dates attached.* Sets **rebuttalFiled**; hidden item: the rebuttal answering the scored summary's attendance error in writing (Rule 1340(E)/(F) — untested). This is Hinge D: the summary's "service engagement: low" line was scored from data counting the two bus-cut visits as refusals, and judges do not do appendix math uninvited.
5. **The Letter at the Transit Office** — *Request the transit authority letter in writing and get it into the packet.* Sets **busLetter**; hidden item: the transit authority letter showing the route was suspended on the two missed dates. The judge's two direct questions at the review are both about September and about what changed — this letter answers the first with letterhead instead of memory.
6. **Asks You Can Say in One Sentence** — *Learn the three asks until each fits in one sentence, exactly as a judge would say it.* Sets **asksReady**: a § 6351(f)(3) progress finding, unsupervised day visits named in the order, and the next review date set from the bench. Findings are sentences spoken on the record; an ask that cannot be said in one sentence rarely becomes one.
7. **The Agency Presents "Minimal"** — *Counsel offers the packet and the agency's own visit logs — exhibits, handed up and marked.* This choice is flag-gated: it requires **exhibitsReady** + **recordsRequested** both set. This is Hinge E: the court now makes its determinations looking at admitted numbers — the agency's logs against the agency's adjective — instead of at the report alone.
8. **The Determinations, Said Aloud** — *The court finds substantial compliance and substantial progress, and orders the next step.* This choice is flag-gated: it requires **exhibitsReady**, **recordsRequested**, **providerProof**, **rebuttalFiled**, **busLetter**, and **asksReady**, with **adjectiveFight** not set; hidden item: the order with the finding under § 6351(f)(3). It leads to **The Record Says Otherwise**.

Supporting route choices that cost nothing and keep the record honest: counting the folder's real numbers at **What the Folder Says** (22 of 24, course complete, 24 of 26) before counsel ever sees the report, and answering the judge's two questions at **Two Questions From the Bench** with dates rather than feelings — dates are the dialect findings are written in.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **The court, not the report, makes the determinations.** A permanency hearing is held within six months of removal and within six months of each previous one (§ 6351(e)(3)(i)). At it, the court determines the continuing necessity of placement, compliance with the permanency plan, and progress toward alleviating the circumstances that caused placement (§ 6351(f)(1),(2),(3)), and whether reasonable efforts were made to finalize the plan (§ 6351(f)(5.1)). "Minimal" is none of those determinations — it is an adjective in a document, and it controls the outcome only when nothing admissible contradicts it.
- **Evidence controls only when it is offered.** A finding is built from what was handed up, marked, and admitted. Steps 2, 3, and 7 are that single mechanic repeated: copies with an index can be offered; originals in a tote bag cannot; a provider's dated attendance record can be weighed; a warm letter cannot be cross-examined or counted.
- **Agency records are obtainable on request** — the record "shall be released upon request" (55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)), and the rule is not to be read to protect agency employees' privacy as a basis for withholding (§ 3130.44(f)). Step 1 is that right exercised in writing, early enough for the answer to arrive before the hearing. The logs it produced were the agency's own refutation of the agency's own adjective.
- **A scored summary's factual errors can be answered — with the flag flying.** The route files a written rebuttal presenting evidence and argument against the summary's attendance line and challenging its factual error (Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(E), (F)). Every use of that step in this scenario carries its caveat: whether Rule 1340 reaches a scored assessment summary has NOT been tested in a published opinion — it is an argument counsel makes, not a settled right. The rebuttal wins its place not by the rule's certainty but by putting the correction, with dates, in the court's hands before the hearing either way.
- The order the route earns — substantial findings, the goal kept at reunification, a named next step, a date — is the permanency system working as designed: determinations from evidence, in the open, on the record.

## (4) The traps

1. **The tote bag at "Copies, Tabs, an Index"** — bringing the originals unoffered because they are "the real documents." Cost: no **exhibitsReady**, and the win and partial endings both close — the findings choice to **"Minimal, Unchallenged"** (the loss) is the only one left even if the records were requested. The certificates existed the whole time, in a bag, which is not a place evidence lives.
2. **Arguing the adjective at "The Agency Presents 'Minimal'"** — spending the hearing on whether "minimal" is an unfair word instead of offering numbers. Cost: **adjectiveFight** is set and the win is barred outright; the best remaining outcome is the **partial** ending, **"'Minimal' Becomes 'Moderate.'"** Eleven minutes of argument produced no exhibit and moved no number.
3. **Skipping the rebuttal at "The Error in the Appendix"** — hoping the judge notices that the scored summary counted bus-cut visits as refusals. Cost: no **rebuttalFiled**, so the win closes and the outcome is capped at **partial** (**"'Minimal' Becomes 'Moderate'"**): the "low engagement" line survives the hearing unanswered and goes on quoting itself into the next report.
4. **Asking for the record by phone at "The File You Have Never Seen"** — accepting "I'll pull what I can." Cost: no **recordsRequested**, the visit logs never arrive, the full offer at the review is gated shut, and the outcome is capped at **partial** at best: Deanna's memory weighed against a written report, with the agency's own "engaged, prepared" sitting unread in a file she never obtained.
5. **The letter instead of the records at "The Letter Problem"** — and, its twin, explaining September from memory at "The Letter at the Transit Office." Costs: no **providerProof** / no **busLetter**, and each independently caps the outcome at **partial**. Kind words and true stories are both things a judge can believe and neither is a thing she can build a finding from.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The judge's temperament on the morning. The caseworker who chose the adjective and the supervisor who let it stand. Whether the transit authority's letter takes four days or three weeks, whether a fax machine works, whether the worker who wrote "engaged, prepared" twenty-four times is the worker whose summary gets read aloud. The route above controls none of that — it only guarantees that whatever the uncontrollable does, it has to do it in a room where the numbers are also present, in a form the court can use. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, judges, resources, and luck as much as on any parent's choices. The strategy's whole claim is narrower: the parts that *were* Deanna's — a request in writing, copies instead of a tote bag, records instead of a letter, an error answered before it was adopted — were done as paper, on time, where a judge could take them.
