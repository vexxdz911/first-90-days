**Traps traced to endings:**

- **Treating the identity error as an insult instead of an identifiers problem.** The winning route demands the identifiers in writing (full name, date of birth, report number), supplies the cousin's distinguishing identifiers from the family's own records, and requires the correction to be transmitted to *every* holder of the wrong summary — county placement unit, study file, vendor, state extract — with written confirmations. A correction that reaches three of four holders is a rumor with good intentions.
- **Anger in ink.** The sponsor's rule governs every letter: someday a hearing officer reads them to decide who you are.
- **"Ending — One File Corrected (partial)":** Lev placed with Gregory; the report stands. The guide holds the two facts apart on purpose — the kinship track and the appeal track are separate files with separate burdens, and winning one does not launder the other.
- **"Ending — Upheld and Waiting":** both tracks stall — the appeal's judgment lands against the family and the study correction is still traveling. Traced to the time-shape of every mechanism: clocks run whether or not anyone feels ready.

**What no strategy controls:** the hearing officer (another officer could find the narrative sufficient on the same record — Act 119's burden shift raises the floor; it does not appoint the judge) and the dark luck at the scenario's center: had the conflated report been Gregory's own, the study's question would have been real and this ending unavailable. The strategy's claim is narrower and complete: both files now say what is true, and courts can work with true files.
