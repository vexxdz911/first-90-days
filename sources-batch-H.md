## SCENARIO 57 — "The Referral That Never Came" (18 nodes, 2 endings)

*A mother whose daughter is home under court supervision is ordered into a named parenting program; the worker says the referral was sent; the program has no record of her; five weeks later a progress note calls her "not yet engaged in parenting services."*

- **The services / referral-gap pattern documented across the published termination-of-parental-rights opinions in the project library:** compliance is measured at review and at termination against the services the *record* shows were ordered — the case-plan objective sets catalogued in `sources-and-alterations.md`'s Scenario 3 discussion of **In the Interest of J.L.L., No. 1714 EDA 2023 (Pa. Super. Apr. 26, 2024)** (parenting classes, treatment, employment, housing as plan objectives, with compliance rated from the record) supply the texture of a plan whose lines are later read as facts about the parent.
- **In re T.J.J.M. (Pa. Super. 2018)**, as catalogued in the same discussion: a parent willing to engage whose services were **not fitted to his circumstances** — used here only for its recurring shape, services that exist on the plan's page but not, in usable form, in the parent's life. Scenario 57 is that shape with the failure moved one step earlier: the service is not mis-fitted but never transmitted at all, the mildest and most ordinary version of the documented gap, and the one most completely provable from paper.

### Beats drawn
The plan ordering a named program; the worker's verbal assurance that a referral "was sent"; the program's empty file; the five-week silence; the progress note rating the parent against the unstarted service ("not yet engaged"); and a review that weighs the note. Each beat is the documented pattern's beat, re-costumed.

### Alterations

## SCENARIO 60 — "The New Worker" (28 nodes, 3 endings, Hard)

*A mother eight months into her son's placement gets a new caseworker. The incoming worker inherits a summary with two stale lines — a completed service listed as open, a passed screen listed as missed — and, because the file copy is a draft, re-orders an assessment she already completed.*

### Documented pattern basis

Worker turnover and file-driven continuity loss, as documented across the published opinions in the project library. The pattern, stated as a pattern: later readers of a case — a successor worker, a report writer, an appellate court — meet only the file, and the file's errors and omissions become the case they act on. Two library textures carry the pattern most directly, and both are already documented in `sources-and-alterations.md`:

1. **In re Adoption of A.P.C.-D., No. 1432 MDA 2025 (Pa. Super. 2026)** — an appellate court parsing, line by line, which findings the certified record actually supported. Drawn on here for the discipline behind the scenario's spine: a new reader of the case can act only on what the record contains, so a stale summary line is not a misunderstanding to be talked away but a text to be corrected in writing. (Cited in `sources-and-alterations.md`, Scenario 3 sources, item 4.)
2. **Mulholland v. Government of Berks County (3d Cir. 2013)** — the footnote texture, noted in `sources-and-alterations.md` (Scenario 2 sources, item 7): a caseworker testifying years later with no independent recollection of the investigation, testifying solely from the form. Drawn on here for the successor worker's posture: Mr. Wilkes is not careless and not a villain; he reads what the file gives him to read, exactly as the Mulholland footnote's witness could only read what the form gave her.

Supporting pattern texture, from the same library: the repeated appearance in published opinions of parents whose compliance was summarized at a level the underlying record did not support, and corrected — if at all — only when primary paper (certificates, sign-in sheets, dated letters) was put into the record. The scenario's two stale lines are invented instances of that documented shape, not any case's lines.

### Beats drawn

- The goodbye letter and the handoff summary as the new worker's only map (pattern basis above, item 1).
- The two stale lines surfacing conversationally at the first meeting — a completed parenting program listed as open; a passed screen listed as missed (invented instances of the documented summary-vs.-record gap).
- The re-ordered assessment whose sole defect is that the file copy is a draft (the form-as-record texture, item 2: an unsigned working copy cannot testify to a completion).
- The parent's own binder, kept in date order from month one, as the parallel file (documented pattern across the project library: primary paper outlasts summaries).
- The one-page written status letter with certificates sent in the new worker's first weeks (the orientation move: primary documents arriving before the summary sets).
- The record request under § 3130.44(d) surfacing the *draft* review report — template lines and all — while it is still correctable.
- The written correction, filed with attachments before the review, versus the same correction raised orally at a meeting (the library's recurring filed-vs.-spoken contrast).

### Alterations

All source matters involve different family structures, postures, and procedural stages (appeals, expunction hearings, termination trials). None of their facts is used. The composite is one mother (Marta), one son (Silas, 9), an outgoing worker (Ms. Renshaw), an incoming worker (Mr. Wilkes), and the mother's lawyer (Ms. Parr) — all invented, as are the program, the screen, the assessment, the summary's two lines, and every document quoted or described. ## SCENARIO 61 — "The Expired Referral" (27 nodes, 3 endings, Hard)

*A sequential plan: Step A (a trauma-therapy evaluation) must be completed before Step B (the parenting program) will enroll her. Sequential-services and referral mechanics, as documented practice patterns in published termination-of-parental-rights opinions' compliance discussions already in the project library — stated as patterns, never as holdings:

1. **In the Interest of J.L.L., No. 1714 EDA 2023 (Pa. Super. Apr. 26, 2024)** — the case-plan objective set (parenting classes, treatment, employment, housing) against which compliance is later measured item by item. Drawn on here for the sequential plan's structure: a plan written as an ordered set of objectives, where an early objective's non-completion is later read as the parent's compliance failure without regard to what produced it. (Cited in `sources-and-alterations.md`, Scenario 3 sources, item 1.)
2. **In re Adoption of N.R.A., No. 915 WDA 2025 (Pa. Super. 2026)** — waitlist testimony: a mother testifying she was on a waitlist, with the delay entering the compliance discussion only through later testimony. Drawn on here for the waitlist as a lived fact that the file records thinly or not at all — the pattern this scenario answers by documenting the wait, and the referral chain around it, as it happens. (Cited in `sources-and-alterations.md`, Scenario 3 sources, item 2.)

Supporting pattern texture, from the same library and from Scenario 14's documented basis: services whose availability, capacity, or administrative processing — not the parent's effort — determined when (or whether) a plan step could be completed, with the resulting gap later summarized in reports in grammar that assigns the delay to the parent ("A not completed" is this scenario's invented instance of that documented sentence-shape).

- The expiry playing out differently by the paper: with the dated referral and the nudge, the provider honors the original queue position on the re-send as a continuation; without them, the queue restarts silently and the re-referral is a new arrival.

## SCENARIO 62 — "The Visit Notes" (26 nodes, 3 endings)

*A mother in supervised visits learns that the visit notes are the case's memory: one note calls her "flat" after a double shift and is quoted, three reviews later in story terms, as a "possible impairment concern"; another note records a climbing-frame fall as "bruise observed… mother offered no explanation initially," although she reported it immediately. Her counters are her own same-day notes, the record request, and a written correction filed into the record.*

### Documented pattern basis (from the project's existing research only)

- **Visit-note and report language hardening into findings, across the published opinions in the project library.** The recurring documented pattern these scenarios are built from is that hearings and reviews are conducted from **contemporaneous notes and forms** — testimony and reports built from what was written at the time, quoted forward from document to document. Two library anchors, already recorded in `sources-and-alterations.md` (Scenario 2's source list):
  - **J.S. v. Commonwealth, Department of Public Welfare (Pa. Cmwlth. 1989)** — a caseworker's **contemporaneous observations carried into the hearing record** years of procedure later. Drawn on for the premise: what the supervisor writes in the room is what later rooms will treat as the event.
  - **Mulholland v. Government of Berks County (3d Cir. 2013)** — the footnote recording that the caseworker, testifying years later, had **no recollection of the investigation and testified solely from the CY-48 form**. Drawn on for the complementary premise: **the file outlives memory** — which is why the parent's own contemporaneous notes, and a correction filed *into* the file, are the scenario's whole strategy.
- **55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(f) — previously verified in this project.** The use here follows the reading recorded as previously verified in this project from the 55 Pa. Code text and used in v1 (Scenario 31's research line; verbatim text recorded in `sources-batch-E.md`): the release rule is **not to be read to protect agency employees' privacy**, and it is used in Scenario 62 for exactly that and nothing more — as the answer when a supervisor's visit observations are blacked out as "staff privacy." No broader proposition about § 3130.44(f) is stated or relied on.

### Beats drawn

- The note as the case's memory; summaries quoting notes, and reports quoting summaries, with wording drifting at each quotation ("flat" → "possible impairment concern"; an explanation recorded on the note's second page never quoted).
- The minor fall at a visit, reported immediately by the parent, recorded in a form that makes the report invisible ("offered no explanation initially") — texture from the library's injury-documentation pattern (J.S.'s contested caretaker/medical characterizations), reduced to a scraped arm and kept procedural: a sleeve held, a bandage negotiated. No injury detail beyond that.
- The redacted packet and the one-line wrong date (an attended visit marked missed) — documented-pattern composites of how release packets and logs actually arrive, drawn from the project's record-request scenarios (notably Scenario 31's redaction beats), not from any single case.
- The correction filed *into* the record, so the entry and its answer travel together — the Mulholland lesson inverted: if the file outlives memory, put the answer where the file lives.

### Alterations applied

No published case in the library is a "flat visit note" case, and none is reenacted. The composite merges the library's contemporaneous-note pattern (J.S.), its file-outlives-memory footnote (Mulholland), and the project's own redaction/record-request mechanics into an invented visit-room story. Mother (Felicia), child (Ezra, 6), supervisor (Ms. Goss), and caseworker (Mr. Henley) are invented; the double shift, the climbing frame, the clock drawing, the missed-date error, and all dialogue are invented. No county, facility name, or employer is given.

In-body dates are computed by the generator from the start date, never hand-typed, including: the "flat" visit, **Thursday, July 9, 2026** (dayOffset 3); the good visit, **Monday, July 13, 2026** (dayOffset 7); the visit wrongly marked missed, **Thursday, July 23, 2026** (dayOffset 17); the review, dayOffset 73 (**Thursday, September 17, 2026**); all three endings at dayOffset 80 (**Thursday, September 24, 2026**). Day offsets are non-decreasing along every edge (validated).

### Law used

42 Pa.C.S. § 6337 (counsel); 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d) (records released on request); 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(f) (as recorded above, v1 wording only). Everything else is generic process description, uncited. ASFA (42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E)) appears only in `strategy-62.md`'s framing paragraph, record-dependent, with no date computed.

### Withholdings
