**Traps traced to endings:**

- **Fighting ink before arithmetic:** the redactions are annoying and secondary. The winning route reads the items first, finds Item 14, and lets § 3130.44(f) collect the rater's name in due course. A family that leads with the redaction fight arrives at the conference with a grievance instead of a recomputation.
- **The re-score offer** (`agency_concede`): administratively fixing the number would dissolve the hearing and the stipulation with it. Declined — gratefully, in writing — because a correction won by stipulation in an order binds every future reader of the file; an office correction is a rumor by spring.
- **"Ending — The Move That Wasn't (partial)":** the arithmetic wins, no alternative is ready to be ordered; Otto stays in his current home and the kinship question is relisted in thirty days.
- **"Ending — Decided on a Summary":** the unexamined assessment rules the hearing by default. Its debrief enumerates the toolkit step by step — every step days of work and dollars of postage — because that is the scenario's transferable content.

**What no strategy controls:** the rater could have been right. If the certified logs had shown three real redirections, this identical toolkit produces a fair loss — the same memorandum, the same stipulation, the opposite arithmetic. The discipline is a machine for making the court decide on the true record, which is why it is worth building without knowing what the record will say.
