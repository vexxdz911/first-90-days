# Strategy Guide — Scenario 74: "Goal: Changed?"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "Goal Held — Reunification, With a Date."**

State it honestly: this is a **win**, and it is also a schedule, not a homecoming party. The agency's request to change Quinn's permanency goal from reunification to adoption is denied, and the court orders a stepped return under 42 Pa.C.S. § 6351(f.1)(1) — unsupervised day visits in January, overnights by February, full return by the spring review, with services and screens continuing through every step. Quinn does not walk out of the courthouse on December 17. What Josie wins is the thing the hearing could actually give: the plan aims at home again, the return has court-ordered dates, and the order's reasonable-efforts finding records, in the judge's own dry sentence, that the eleven-week housing delay was not the mother's. The labeled partial ending — "Goal Held — The Clock Named Aloud" — holds the goal but grants no schedule, and is scored as partial in section (4) below.

## (2) The route

Verified by walking the scenario graph from the start node (see the verification note at the end). Key decision points, in order — node title, winning choice, and what it changes:

1. **Hinge A: The Written Request** — *Sign the written request for the complete agency record, through counsel, today.* Sets **recordsRequested**; hidden item: the agency record on written request (§ 3130.44(d)), which dates the housing referral March 4 (drafted) and May 27 (sent). This is the single highest-leverage move in the scenario: without it, the referral delay is an assertion, and the win closing is flag-gated shut.
2. **The Guardian ad Litem's Voice** — *Ask him exactly what he wants to see in the packet before he commits to a schedule.* Sets **galSupport**. Quinn's own lawyer supports more time on the record — if the record he is shown is the one described.
3. **Hinge B: A Packet With an Index** — *Build the full packet — five exhibits, one-page index, four copies.* Cash −20. Sets **exhibitsReady**; hidden item: the progress packet with index (treatment, aftercare, screens, visits, lease). A pile persuades nobody; numbered exhibits answer the § 6351(f)(2) compliance and (f)(3) progress determinations in findable form.
4. **Aftercare, in Writing** — *Ask Ms. Tran to include the aftercare dates, not just the completion date.* Sets **providerLetter**. "Progress" becomes a chronology instead of an adjective.
5. **Hinge C: Will She Testify?** — *Ask Lori to testify live by video, home study in hand.* Sets **kinTestifies**; at the hearing this produces the hidden item: maternal aunt testified, completed home study filed. Her presence puts a fit and willing relative (§ 6351(f.1)) on the table and kills the "adoption is the only permanency" argument.
6. **Eleven Weeks, in Two Dates** — *Build a one-page timeline — drafted, sent, lease signed — and put it behind the index.* Sets **referralDated**. March 4, May 27, June lease: the § 6351(f)(5.1) reasonable-efforts argument, laid down as dates rather than accusations.
7. **Hinge D: The Stale Report** — *File the short written correction through counsel before the hearing, proof attached.* Sets **casaCorrected**; hidden item: written correction to the CASA report, filed before the hearing. The five-month-old report is corrected as a document, in the judge's packet, before the hearing — never by attacking the volunteer in the room.
8. **One Line in the Scored Summary** — *File the rebuttal with the lease attached, Rule 1340 flagged as untested in the filing itself.* Sets **summaryRebutted**. The scored summary's stale "housing: unstable" line is answered with the lease as proof and Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(E), (F) as an argument — labeled in the filing as what it is: whether Rule 1340 reaches a scored assessment summary has NOT been tested in a published opinion.
9. **Answering a Clock That Has Not Struck** — *Prepare the answer: the (f)(9) determination is not triggered at month ten — and the plan's time frames cut both ways.* Sets **clockAnswered**. The agency will invoke the fifteen-month date; the route names it, dates it, and answers it instead of hiding from it.
10. **Paper, Filed Before the Hearing** — *Send the GAL a courtesy copy of the full packet today, and keep the mailing receipt.* Cash −10. Sets **packetShared**. An ally who has read the packet is a different witness from one handed it in the hallway.
11. **The Ask, With Dates** — *Draft the ask as a stepped return with dates — overnights by February, full return by the spring review.* Sets **steppedAsk**. This is the third flag the win closing requires: (f.1)(1) drawn as a schedule a judge can sign, answering the true sentence "Quinn's life is not on hold" with dates instead of open-ended time.
12. **What Is Different This Time?** (rehearsal) — *Answer it with the record — exhibit by exhibit, date by date.* Sets **answerReady**. When the judge asks the same question for real, the answer is a paper trail, not a vow.
13. **The Permanency Hearing** — *Take the bus in early, packet on your lap.* Cash −10. The route's total spending is $40; you finish with $130 of the $170 start.
14. **The Voice From Ohio** — *Lori testifies live by video, and her completed home study is handed up as she speaks.* Flag-gated on **kinTestifies**. She supports Quinn going home to his mother first — and stands as the (f.1) relative option herself.
15. **Correcting a Document, Not a Person** — *Counsel addresses the paper, not the person: "The report was accurate when written; the correction and its proof were filed Monday, Your Honor."* This choice matters to the gate: attacking the volunteer sets **casaAttacked**, which closes the win closing and caps the case at partial.
16. **Determinations First** (the closing) — *Close in sequence: (f)(2) compliance, (f)(3) progress, (f)(5.1) the unsent referral — then (f.1)(1), the stepped return with dates.* This choice is flag-gated: it is only available with **recordsRequested** + **exhibitsReady** + **steppedAsk** all set and **casaAttacked** not set. It adds the hidden items: the § 6351(f)(2), (3), (5.1) determinations made on the record, and the stepped return schedule with dates. It leads to **Goal Held — Reunification, With a Date**.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **A goal change is an evidence fight with a statutory sequence.** At a permanency hearing — held within six months of removal and within six months of each previous permanency hearing (§ 6351(e)(3)(i)) — the court must determine the continuing necessity of placement, compliance with the permanency plan (§ 6351(f)(2)), progress toward alleviating the circumstances that caused placement (§ 6351(f)(3)), and whether reasonable efforts were made to finalize the plan (§ 6351(f)(5.1)). Only based upon those determinations and all relevant evidence does the court choose the outcome under § 6351(f.1): return to parent; adoption (agency to file for termination); legal custodian; fit and willing relative; or another planned permanent living arrangement. The route wins the determinations first and the outcome second, because that is the order the statute argues in.
- **The record was the agency's own, obtained the way the regulation provides.** Agency records "shall be released upon request" (55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)), and the rule is not to be read to protect agency employees' privacy as a basis for withholding (§ 3130.44(f)). The written request is what turned the unsent referral from a suspicion into two dates the agency could not dispute — and those dates are reasonable-efforts material under § 6351(f)(5.1), because part of the housing delay the agency blamed on the mother was the agency's.
- **The fifteen-month clock was answered honestly, which means it was not conceded early.** At month ten, the § 6351(f)(9) determination — whether a child in placement at least 15 of the last 22 months has an agency termination filing behind him, unless a relative cares for him, a compelling reason is documented, or the family was not provided the services necessary for safe return within the plan's time frames — has not been triggered. Pennsylvania's implementation of 42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E) schedules that determination for month fifteen. The route's answer borrows nothing from May: it wins December on December's determinations, and notes that the services principle inside (f)(9)(iii) already casts its shadow through the (f)(5.1) efforts finding.
- **A goal change is not a termination, in either direction.** Granting it would not have ended Josie's rights that day; denying it does not end the case. Termination, if it ever comes, is its own proceeding requiring clear and convincing evidence — which is exactly why preserving the record (exhibits offered, corrections filed, the aunt's study on file) matters in every ending, including the losing one.
- **The scored summary was rebutted as an argument, not oversold as a right.** The rebuttal invoked Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(E) (present evidence and argument to rebut a provision) and (F) (factual errors may be challenged), and said in its own text that whether Rule 1340 reaches a scored assessment summary has NOT been tested in a published opinion. The lease did the proving; the rule was only the door knocked on.

## (4) The traps

1. **Skipping the written record request at "Hinge A: The Written Request"** — asking the caseworker by phone to "send the important parts." Cost: no **recordsRequested**. The referral delay stays unproven, the (f)(5.1) argument loses its only documentary basis, and the win closing is flag-gated shut — the case is capped at **Goal Held — The Clock Named Aloud** (partial) no matter how strong everything else is.
2. **Bringing a pile instead of a packet at "Hinge B: A Packet With an Index."** Cost: no **exhibitsReady**, and the same gate closes. Eight months of real progress that cannot be found in thirty seconds is, functionally, eight months the court never saw — argue the goal in the abstract at the closing and this trap runs all the way to **Goal Changed — The Record She Built**.
3. **Asking for "more time" instead of a stepped return at "The Ask, With Dates."** Cost: no **steppedAsk**, gate closed. The agency's truest sentence — Quinn's life is not on hold — goes unanswered, because open-ended time is the one ask this hearing was built to refuse. The goal may still be held, but with no schedule the judge sets the tempo herself, by naming the fifteen-month date aloud: the partial ending.
4. **Attacking the CASA volunteer personally at "Correcting a Document, Not a Person."** Cost: **casaAttacked** set; the win closing closes even with every other flag in place. The room's sympathy moves to the volunteer, the court's confidence in counsel's judgment cools, and the stale report — never corrected on paper — does its work anyway. This trap produces the partial ending.
5. **Hiding from the clock at "Answering a Clock That Has Not Struck."** Cost: the agency's month-fifteen argument stands in the room unanswered, and the judge names the date anyway — on her terms, in the order, as the case's tempo. Unanswered clocks do not get quieter; they get adopted. Partial, or worse.

The partial ending, honestly labeled: **"Goal Held — The Clock Named Aloud"** is a partial win — the goal stays reunification, but there is no return schedule, the judge names the fifteen-month date (next May, May 2027) on the record, and a ninety-day review runs against a spoken deadline. Everything the win route builds can still be built in those ninety days; it just has to be built faster, against a clock the whole courtroom has now heard.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The judge's weighing of a real relapse against eight real months of recovery — another judge can land the other way on the identical record, and the route does not pretend otherwise. The GAL's judgment, which was his to give or withhold. Whether the produced record contains the referral page at all; files lose pages, and a request can only surface what exists. The caseworker's candor on cross, the aunt's wifi in Ohio, the foster placement's stability, and the plain luck of which packet a busy judge reads first. The route controls none of that. It controls only this: that when the uncontrollable happened, it happened to a record — requested in writing, indexed, corrected, and asked for in the statute's own order. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, judges, resources, and luck as much as on any parent's choices. The strategy's whole claim is narrower: the parts that were Josie's to build were built before the hearing, on paper, with dates.
