# Strategy Guide — Scenario 76: "The Last Fifteen Minutes"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "The Exception, Documented."**

State it honestly: this is the scenario's true win, and it is still not a reunification scene. The court makes the § 6351(f)(9)(ii) and (f)(9)(iii) findings **on the record**; temporary legal custody goes to Grandma Rose under § 6351(a)(2)(i) as a bridge; the goal stays reunification with a dated, stepped return running from her house. Kian and Zara do **not** come home to Miriam at the end of this ending — they cross to their grandmother's kitchen, mid-bridge. The win is what happens (and doesn't happen) on Thursday, January 20, 2028: the fifteen-month date arrives and meets a determination already made, documented, and dictated, instead of a filing track. The two lesser endings are labeled as what they are — **"A Bridge, Not a Return"** is a **partial** win (children with Rose, goal held, but no (f)(9) findings made and the January date still to survive), and **"The Findings Nobody Asked For"** is a **partial-loss** (a good hearing undone by an uncorrected written order).

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **The 15-of-22 determination and its exceptions.** If a child has been in placement at least 15 of the last 22 months, the court determines whether the agency has filed (or joined) a termination petition **unless** (i) the child is cared for by a relative, (ii) the agency has documented a compelling reason filing would not serve the child's needs and welfare, or (iii) the family was not provided the services necessary for safe return within the plan's time frames — 42 Pa.C.S. § 6351(f)(9), Pennsylvania's implementation of the federal rule at 42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E). An exception is a *determination the court makes on documentation*, not a favor — which is why Steps 2, 3, and 6 exist: the packet aims at (ii) **and** (iii), in writing, filed before the date, with the file's own pages attached.
- **The agency's own record is obtainable on request.** The record "shall be released upon request" — 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d) — and the rule is not to be read to protect agency employees' privacy as a basis for withholding — § 3130.44(f). Step 2 is that right exercised specifically, and Step 5 is what it buys: the referral log, in the agency's words, saying *queued — not sent*.
- **The permanency determinations are the court's, not the report's.** At each permanency hearing the court must determine continuing necessity of placement, plan compliance, progress toward alleviating the circumstances that caused placement, and whether reasonable efforts were made to finalize the plan — § 6351(f)(1), (2), (3), (5.1). The findings list in Step 7 is those determinations, plus the (f)(9) pair, written as asks before a tired 4 p.m. dictation could shrink them.
- **The bridge is a real disposition.** Temporary legal custody may be placed with a qualified individual, including a relative — § 6351(a)(2)(i) — and the permanency outcomes in § 6351(f.1) include the fit-and-willing-relative outcome. But courts rarely grant placements nobody asks for: Steps 4 and 8 are the ask, made in writing beforehand and on the record in the room.
- **Findings belong on the record, and the order must match them** — § 6351(b). Step 9 is the discipline that follows: specific findings are asked for aloud at the close, corrections are made while the transcript is still warm, and the dictated visit numbers — not the word "weekly" — are what the written order is later drafted from.
- **A petition is never an outcome.** Termination of parental rights, if it ever came, would require clear and convincing evidence at its own hearing. The documented exception is what keeps January 20, 2028 from becoming that fight at all.

## (4) The traps

1. **Packet-as-speech at "Filed, or Spoken"** — keeping the exception packet as counsel's oral argument because she'll "say it all on Tuesday." Cost: no **packetFiled**, and the win gate at **May the Record Reflect** closes — **end_partial at best** ("A Bridge, Not a Return"), with the court "noting" an argument instead of finding an exception on a document it read in chambers.
2. **The silent study at "Making the Ask About Rose"** — leaving Grandma Rose's completed, approved home study unmentioned because "everyone knows about Rose." Cost: no **kinshipAsked**; the court discusses continued foster care as if it were the only geography. Traced to **"A Bridge, Not a Return"** (partial) where other paper saves the placement informally, and to **"Fifteen of Twenty-Two"** (loss) where nothing does. Knowing is not asking.
3. **Hallway findings at "Anything Further From Counsel?"** — letting the dictated errors stand with "we'll fix it when the written order comes." Cost: the order arrives on Day 21 with visits misstated as "weekly" and the packet "noted," not found — **"The Findings Nobody Asked For"** (partial-loss), where counsel must move to correct paper that a four-minute request in the room would have prevented, with January five weeks away.
4. **Treating January as next year's problem at "Two Clocks"** — getting through the hearing and the holidays first. Cost: everything. No request, no packet, no notice, no list — the drift branch reaches the same hearing with only the agency's report in the room, and ends at **"Fifteen of Twenty-Two"**: the court signals the filing track, the agency files in January, and the exception the file itself supported was never put in front of the court.
5. **Rehearsing from memory at "The List, in Order"** — trusting that counsel knows what to ask for without the page. Cost: no **findingsList**, so the gated choice at **Anything Further From Counsel?** never appears; the hearing closes in silence at "weekly" and "notes the packet," and the route ends in the partial or the loss depending on what else December built.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The judge's reading of "compelling." Whether the correction is granted graciously, grudgingly, or not at all. What the referral log actually says — in another December it says "sent," and (iii) is honestly lost and the case rides on (ii) and Rose. The agency's filing choices, the children's own weather in a new kitchen, a caseworker's caseload, a court reporter's sick day, and the plain luck of which judge draws the list on a Tuesday in December. The route above controls none of that. It controls only this: that when the last fifteen minutes arrive, the parent is standing on the platform with the list in hand — and that January 20, 2028 meets a record, not a silence. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, judges, and luck as much as on any parent's choices; the strategy's whole claim is the narrower one December makes, four pieces of paper at a time.
