# SOURCES AND ALTERATIONS — Batch E: Scenarios 29–36 (Complex tier, incl. capstone)

*How "The First 90 Days" Scenarios 29–36 were built from the published public record and the project's verified research, what was drawn from each source, what was changed, and which legal propositions were newly verified before use.*

**Status:** Draft documentation, for internal development and attorney review. Nothing here or in the scenarios is published or sent.

**Source rule applied:** Every scenario derives only from published Pennsylvania Superior Court, Commonwealth Court, and Supreme Court opinions in dependency, termination-of-parental-rights, and CPSL expunction matters, from official statutory/regulatory text, and (Scenario 32 only) the project's own verified Universal Assessment research. No private case, file, docket, or person from the development team's own orbit was used as source material, in any form.

**Alteration doctrine applied:** Each scenario is a composite. The procedural spine and testimony-textured beats come from the published sources; names, ages, counties, occupations, and identifying facts are changed, and beats from at least two published sources are merged in every scenario (three or more in the capstone), so that no real family maps onto any character.

**Engine-flag note (all eight scenarios):** `recordsRequested` is set in every scenario. `removed` is set **only** where a removal actually occurs inside the playable story — Scenarios 30 (removal branch), 31 (opening scene), 34 (opening scene), and 36 (opening scene) — and is deliberately absent from Scenarios 29, 32, 33, and 35, where placement predates the story; setting it there would fire the engine's 72-hour shelter-care banner falsely.

**New law verified in this batch:** Only the Scenario 31 research round added primary-text verifications (§ 3130.44(d) and (f); Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(A), (B)(1), (E), (F)) — quoted in full under Scenario 31 below. All other scenarios used the shared verified set only. No scenario asserts any UA-specific court holding; Rule 1340's application to the Universal Assessment is flagged in-game as untested wherever it is invoked.

## SCENARIO 29 — "The Teen Who Says No" (33 nodes, 4 endings)

*A 15-year-old refuses the placement and the services aimed at her; her mother's case runs through the teen's own voice, and the playable skill is what a parent can still put on the record when the plan's subject won't cooperate.*

### Published sources used

1. **In the Interest of C.T., No. 1076 WDA 2015 (Pa. Super. Jan. 6, 2016)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2016/1076-wda-2015.html
   - *Beats drawn:* a ~14-year-old who repeatedly refuses to remain in a parent's custody and runs each time he is returned; placement driven by the youth's own refusals; a parent's argument that the dispositional plan gave him nothing he could do — the texture for a plan that assigns the teen's part to the mother.
2. **In re Civil Contempt, Appeal of A.G., No. 1568 EDA 2023 (Pa. Super. Sept. 24, 2024)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2024/1568-eda-2023.html
   - *Beats drawn:* a child who ran from placement to an aunt's home, ran again back to his mother, was returned and ran again, and ran amid courthouse confusion — the placement-instability beats and the aunt-as-chosen-refuge beat.
3. **In the Interest of W.S., No. 805 EDA 2021 (Pa. Super. Nov. 2, 2021)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2021/805-eda-2021.html
   - *Beats drawn:* the youth himself, through counsel, arguing foster care was harming him and he was "doing better" at home; the trial court "cognizant of W.S.'s desire to return home" while deciding otherwise — re-costumed as the quote-vs.-summary problem: the teen's own words existing on the record but reframed by the file.

### Alterations

All source cases involve boys in different family structures. The composite is a mother (Diane) and a 15-year-old daughter (Kayla), placement with a foster caregiver predating the story, a walkout from a family-therapy session, one self-chosen night at an aunt's (Rosa), and one mid-story placement change. Names, ages, genders, counties (none named), occupations, dialogue, and documents are invented; beats from all three sources are merged.

### New law verified

**None.** Verified set only (§ 6337; § 3130.44(d) incl. the full scored assessment; Rule 1340(B)(1) with the untested-as-to-the-assessment flag stated in-game; clear-and-convincing burden).

### Withholdings

(a) 42 Pa.C.S. § 6302(6) "ungovernable/habitual disobedience" dependency — present in *C.T.* but not in the verified set; never cited or used to frame the teen's refusals. (b) The "clear necessity" removal standard in *W.S.* — not used as law. (c) All aging-out specifics — the horizon is generic ("a dependency case cannot stay open forever"), and the withholding is itself stated in the drift ending's debrief. (d) No ASFA date is asserted; placement is only ~7 months old by the endings.

## SCENARIO 30 — "The Third Removal" (33 nodes, 4 endings)

*The title names the threat, not a completed history: a new low-level report lands on a family with two closed cases and one prior voluntary service period, and every professional reads the history before the present facts. The skill is separating THEN from NOW, in writing.*

### Published sources used

1. **In the Interest of S.D. and I.D., 2025 PA Super 79, Nos. 1713–1714 EDA 2024 (Pa. Super. Apr. 8, 2025)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2025/1713-eda-2024.html
   - *Beats drawn:* a dependency petition whose opening paragraphs aver the family's *history* (a 2014 termination as to older children; "a history of not cooperating") ahead of the present report; an investigative assessment reciting that history; a ten-month continuance interval with the children at home and no further allegations — history-as-petition-spine vs. a present-facts interval.
2. **In the Matter of T.D., 2008 PA Super 80 (Pa. Super. Apr. 25, 2008)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2008/s14014-08.html
   - *Beats drawn (¶2):* an extensive history from a child's birth; parents who *agreed to services and the case closed* (1998); a later *school referral for truancy* (2002) reopening involvement — the direct sequence for "closed case + voluntary services + truancy referral read through the old file."
3. **In the Interest of A.B., No. 2495 EDA 2024 (Pa. Super. Apr. 15, 2025)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2025/2495-eda-2024.html
   - *Beats drawn:* agency engagement "since 2015" recited as a list including truancy; a prior adjudication, a finding of *full compliance*, reunification, and discharge of supervision (Feb. 27, 2018), followed years later by a new GPS report — a fully closed, compliant prior case re-read as current risk.

### Alterations

The sources involve substance use, incarceration, physical discipline, and multi-child terminations; none of that is used. The composite is a night-shift warehouse worker (Simone) with two children (Ben, 9; Lily, 6); the new report is deliberately low-level and bruise-free (11 school absences, 7 excused on the school's own printout; "mother sleeps during the day"). Priors are re-costumed as a 2018 housing case (closed), a 2021 *voluntary* support period the mother requested and completed (certificate), and a 2023 case (closed). Stale items are invented: a two-year-old address, a departed cousin still listed as household, and two never-true lines — "declined parenting support sessions (2021)" (contradicted by the completion certificate) and a household member "partner, D." who never lived in the home.

### New law verified

**None.** Verified set only (§ 3130.44(d); § 6337; Rule 1340(B)(1) with untested flag; clear-and-convincing burden; 72-hour shelter care, § 6332 / § 6315(d)).

### Withholdings

(a) *S.D.*'s appellate holding on the scope/timeliness of the dependency inquiry — used only as narrative tension ("whether NOW was ever examined"), never as a rule. (b) No deadline or formal rule invented for correcting a closed file or for voluntary services — the split ending's debrief says expressly that no post-closure correction deadline is claimed, and the voluntary ending treats the written agreement's own terms as the rule. (c) No § 2511/TPR grounds analysed; no termination outcome depicted. (d) No claim that history is inadmissible — the scenario's position is narrower: history must be dated and labelled so the clear-and-convincing showing is made about the present. On the removal branch the clock is exact: removal dayOffset 71 (Tuesday, November 24, 2026) → shelter care hearing dayOffset 74 (Friday, November 27, 2026).

## SCENARIO 31 — "The Confidential File" (35 nodes, 4 endings)

*The records fight itself: a § 3130.44(d) request returns a redacted stack with the scored assessment pages withheld; the parent runs an administrative track and a Rule 1340 court track in parallel, and the endings turn on what the file eventually shows versus what the clock allows.*

### Published sources used

1. **In re A.C., Nos. 54 & 55 EDA 2026 (Pa. Super. Aug. 28, 2026)** (non-precedential memorandum) — https://pacourts.us/assets/opinions/Superior/out/J-A12036-26m%20-%20106905205371922433.pdf?cb=1
   - *Beats drawn:* a parent's Rule 1340 complaint faltering because "No copy of any such request… appears in the certified record"; the court's statement that Rule 1340 substantive interpretation is "an issue of first impression"; disclosures triggered only by request for specifically requested items — the scenario's "your asking is the paper" spine.
2. **In re D.B. & J.M.O.-G., Nos. 190 & 191 MDA 2013 (Pa. Super. Aug. 9, 2013)** (non-precedential) — https://cases.justia.com/pennsylvania/superior-court/190-mda-2013.pdf?ts=1396142370
   - *Beats drawn (procedural texture only):* a CYS-file request litigated as a discovery/release question under Rule 1340, with the juvenile court channeling and limiting release — texture for the protective-order/stated-ground ending. The source's underlying facts were deliberately **not** used.
3. **C.S. v. Commonwealth, Dep't of Human Services, Bureau of Hearings & Appeals, 184 A.3d 600 (Pa. Cmwlth. 2018)** — https://app.midpage.ai/document/cs-v-pa-dhs-bureau-of-hearings-4262515
   - *Beats drawn:* CPSL confidentiality (§ 6339) vs. disclosure duties, including Rule 1800(9)'s suspension of § 6339 only insofar as inconsistent with Rule 1340(B)(1)(e) — the legal weather behind the agency's stated withholding ground.
4. **In re J.W., No. 1507 WDA 2025 (Pa. Super. 2026)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2026/1507-wda-2025.html
   - *Beats drawn:* an agency Rule 1340 undisclosed-evidence fight resolved by examining what the record actually showed was requested/objected to — paper-trail texture.

### Alterations

Composite parent Dana (home-health aide) and daughter Wren, 8; invented county, employer, school, attorney, item facts; the withheld document is a scored assessment (project research), not any source case's file. Source cases' facts (substance exposure, abuse allegations, civil-litigation motive) are not used — only discovery/records procedure and texture, merged from all four sources.

### New law verified from primary text (this batch's only additions)

- **55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)** (official Pa. Code text): *"Information contained in case records shall be released upon request to: (1) Parents and legal guardians. (2) Children's and parents' attorneys…"*
- **55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(f)** (same source): *"Information from a case record may be made available only if the information released does not contain material which violates the right to privacy of another individual or is protected or made confidential by law. This may not be construed to protect the right to privacy of a county agency employe."* — the lawful basis for the game's redactions and stated withholding ground.
- **Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340** (237 Pa. Code ch. 13 official text via pacodeandbulletin.gov, cross-read at Cornell): (A) parties "shall make a good faith effort to resolve all questions of discovery" before moving; (B)(1) "In all cases, on request by a party and subject to any protective order… the county agency shall disclose to a party, all of the following requested items or information, provided they are material to the instant case"; (E) the court "may order such party to permit discovery or inspection, may grant a continuance, or may prohibit such party from introducing evidence or witnesses not disclosed, or… such other order as it deems just"; (F) protective orders "upon a sufficient showing."
- **A.C. (2026)**, quoted in one debrief with its non-precedential status flagged in-game: "there is no case law interpreting Rule 1340 on substantive grounds."

### Withholdings

No day-count is stated anywhere for any appeal of the motion ruling (none was verified). No UA-specific court holding is asserted; the Rule 1340/UA untested flag appears verbatim in both court-track nodes. The removal in the opening scene is exact: Day 0 (Monday, February 2, 2026) → shelter care hearing Day 3 (Thursday, February 5, 2026), § 6332 / § 6315(d).

## SCENARIO 32 — "The Assessment, Appealed" (34 nodes, 4 endings)

*The Universal Assessment scenario, set in an unnamed pilot county. A parent works the Challenge-the-Assessment path: request the full form and item ratings, build an item-by-item rebuttal worksheet from her own records, ask who rated her and what their certification is. The system's answers are partial and procedural; the endings measure what got onto the record.*

### Sources used

1. **In re A.C., Nos. 54 & 55 EDA 2026 (Pa. Super. 2026)** — URL as above. *Beats:* the request must exist in the certified record to be ruled on; Rule 1340 as an issue of first impression — the discipline behind the request-and-log choreography.
2. **In the Interest of M.K.L., No. 947 EDA 2025 (Pa. Super. 2026) (2026 PA Super 33)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2026/947-eda-2025.html
   - *Beats drawn:* permanency reviews tracking compliance item by item on paper; a parent who "had not provided DHS with paystubs to verify her employment since September 2023"; a caseworker testifying she had reviewed the case file — documents as the currency of progress findings.
3. **In re J.J.H., No. 1454 MDA 2017 (Pa. Super. 2018)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2018/1454-mda-2017.html
   - *Beats drawn:* a parent "failed to provide pay stubs for periods of time" held against progress; progress-rating vocabulary ("moderate progress served as a compelling reason") behind the worksheet.
4. **The project's own verified UA research** (the one authorized non-opinion source): PA's Universal Assessment is a PA-specific scored form in the FAST family, with 17 Priority Items whose ratings drive decisions; the item list and scoring rules have never been published. Only Lancaster and Dauphin are publicly confirmable as pilot counties — the scenario's county is therefore kept **unnamed** ("a pilot county").

### Alterations

Composite parent Mara and son Eli, 6, placed four months before the story opens; invented bakery job, landlord, clinic lawyer, items, and documents; no source case's family, allegations, or outcomes reproduced — only review-hearing mechanics and the pay-stub/progress-rating texture.

### New law verified

**None** beyond the shared verified set (§ 3130.44(d) and Rule 1340(B)(1) texts were re-read this round; see Scenario 31).

### Withholdings / honesty rules

The tool is called a scored form throughout; a term scan of the shipped JSON confirms zero instances of "algorithm," "machine learning," "predictive," standalone "AI," "Lancaster," or "Dauphin." No UA-specific holding is asserted; the untested Rule 1340 flag appears in-game and in every ending debrief where Rule 1340 is mentioned. The contents of the eight never-produced ratings are never invented — the unseen ending's debrief expressly declines to fabricate what was never produced. `removed` is deliberately not set: placement predates the story by four months.

## SCENARIO 33 — "The Two Petitions" (36 nodes, 4 endings)

*Concurrent planning in the open: reunification services are still running when the agency moves to change the goal. Two tracks, two finish lines; the skill is litigating the present case while the future case is being built.*

### Published sources used

1. **In re R.J.T., 9 A.3d 1179 (Pa. 2010)** — https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/pa-supreme-court/1550149.html
   - *Beats drawn:* concurrent planning as a dual-track system (quoting *In re Adoption of S.E.G.*, 901 A.2d 1017, 1019 (Pa. 2006)); goal change denied where parents were actively pursuing FSP goals and reunification remained possible; agency counsel's representation that services would continue even if the goal changed; § 6351(f)(9) 15-of-22 framing.
2. **In the Interest of K.C., Jr., No. 667 MDA 2021 (Pa. Super. Nov. 19, 2021)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2021/667-mda-2021.html
   - *Beats drawn:* goal change reversed as premature where the parent had received services only briefly and was affirmatively attending visits, getting screens, and completing intake; the lower court's characterization of reunification as "a fantasy" discounted.
3. **In the Interest of J.W., No. 235 WDA 2020 (Pa. Super. Oct. 19, 2020)** — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2020/235-wda-2020.html
   - *Beats drawn:* the counterweight — goal change to adoption affirmed on a record of moderate-to-minimal compliance; the honest "other column" in the thin-path hearing.
4. **In the Interest of K.M., 2023 PA Super 217** (also a Scenario 34 source) — the services-gap texture: a plan-identified service not actually provided, documented from the agency's own record.

### Alterations

Composite father (Marcus, forklift operator) and daughter (Wren, 7→8); the sources' domestic-violence, incarceration, infant, and multi-child facts discarded. The owed service was re-costumed as a coached-visit referral marked "to be sent" in month 6. Counties, employers, foster family, program, and dialogue invented; beats merged from ≥3 sources. ASFA date machine-computed: placement June 2, 2025 + 15 months = Wednesday, September 2, 2026 (dayOffset 121) — also the goal-change hearing date.

### New law verified

**None.** Concurrent planning's dual-track description is narrative framing drawn from the opinions, not a new primary-text proposition in any debrief.

### Withholdings

No appeal deadline stated anywhere (the goal-change appeal mention is deliberately number-free). No § 2511 grounds analysed. `removed` deliberately not set — placement predates the story (June 2, 2025); the story opens eleven months in.

## SCENARIO 35 — "The Hearing That Wasn't" (36 nodes, 4 endings)

*Continuances stack — each individually reasonable, collectively corrosive — while the ASFA clock does not continue when the hearings do. The skills are objecting on the record in plain words, written readiness filed before every date, and a running chronology that makes delay itself visible in the file.*

### Published sources used (access level stated honestly)

1. **In the Interest of K.F.M.R., No. 1979 EDA 2016 (Pa. Super. July 17, 2017)** — full text read — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2017/1979-eda-2016.html
   - *Beats drawn:* a TPR petition filed July 2013 with the hearing not held until May 26, 2016 "due to a series of continuances"; the child in care 39 months; a continuance denial upheld where the case "had already been continued numerous times" — multi-year slide texture; permanency-vs.-delay framing.
2. **In re K.L.S., No. 1546 MDA 2023 (Pa. Super. 2024)** — search-result excerpts read — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2024/1546-mda-2023.html
   - *Beats drawn:* "it took nearly a year to schedule the TPR hearing"; testimony that "the long duration of these proceedings was having a deleterious effect" — appellate courts remarking on delay as a fact about the child.
3. **Adoption of W.J.R., No. 852 WDA 2019 (Pa. Super. 2019)** — excerpts read — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2019/852-wda-2019.html
   - *Beats drawn:* texture only — how permanency is invoked around continuances. **Not** taught as a rule (see withholdings).
4. **In re Mother (Pa. Super. 2017)** — Studicata summary — https://www.studicata.com/summaries/superior-court-of-pennsylvania/in-re-mother-2017-smib1z/
   - *Beats drawn:* denial of a parent's continuance reversed as an abuse of discretion where no prejudice from delay was shown — the counterweight: delay arguments cut both ways, so objection outcomes are discretion, not entitlement.
5. **In re Adoption of R.J.S., 901 A.2d 502 (Pa. Super. 2006)**, as quoted in later opinions — "a child's life cannot be held in abeyance…" — the child's-clock voice in debriefs.

### Alterations

All source cases are TPR-stage, multi-year matters, several with facts (incarceration, substance use, hospital non-return) not used. The composite is a *review-stage* case: one child (Eli, 7), mother a home-health aide, foster placement, services *completed* — so delay, not parental non-compliance, is the sole corrosive agent. The four continuance reasons (witness unavailable / report not filed → disclosure not filed → courtroom double-booked → counsel conflict) and all dialogue are invented. ASFA clock machine-computed: placement Monday, March 10, 2025 + 15 months = Wednesday, June 10, 2026 (dayOffset 457), stated in-game from the opening node.

### New law verified

**None.** No continuance standard, limit, or objection deadline is stated anywhere as law; debriefs say so explicitly. The scenario teaches only practice against the verified ASFA clock, § 3130.44(d), § 6337, and the clear-and-convincing burden; Rule 1340(B)(1) is invoked at the second hearing with the UA-untested flag.

### Withholdings

The continuance-discretion factor test quoted in *W.J.R.* (prejudice, opposing counsel's willingness, length, complexity) is real case law but was deliberately **not** taught — continuance standards unverified from primary text stay out, and objection is presented purely as practice. No § 2511 grounds or TPR outcome depicted. `removed` deliberately not set (placement predates the story by nine months). Coverage note: *K.L.S.* and *W.J.R.* were read via excerpts/summaries rather than full opinions; every legal proposition used rests only on the pre-verified set, so no proposition depends on an excerpt.

## SCENARIO 36 — "The Whole Table" (Capstone) (40 nodes, 4 composite-state endings)

*Three children, a kinship placement, a CPSL appeal clock, and the ASFA clock in one family at one time. The endings are composite states rather than single outcomes — stated explicitly in `meta.summary` and in each ending's title and debrief — because the three children's arcs resolve differently.*

   - *Beats drawn:* three children (born 2015/2019/2020), emergency custody, a shelter care order placing all three in kinship care with maternal grandparents, a grandmother as the protective, boundary-setting caregiver whose testimony the court credited; clear-and-convincing adjudication analysis child by child — the three-child spine, kinship shelter-care posture, grandmother texture.
2. **In the Interest of J.M.B., No. 1281 EDA 2021 (Pa. Super. 2021)** — excerpts read — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2021/1281-eda-2021.html
   - *Beats drawn:* three children in *different* foster homes, the agency's continuing search for a joint placement, § 6351(f)(5)/(10) sibling-placement considerations — sibling divergence and separate-tracks texture.
3. **In the Interest of L.J.R.A., No. 691 MDA 2024 (Pa. Super. 2024)** — excerpts read — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2024/691-mda-2024.html
   - *Beats drawn:* children in care twenty months across three kinship placements; strong kinship bonds — duration/bond language around the long clock.
4. **In the Interest of J.L.L., No. 1714 EDA 2023 (Pa. Super. Apr. 26, 2024)** — full text read in the Scenario 3 research round (see `sources-and-alterations.md`) — https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2024/1714-eda-2023-0.html
   - *Beats drawn:* case-plan compliance ratings and visit-log texture.
5. **CPSL track mechanics** carried from Scenario 2's verified research (*F.R. v. DPW*; *K.C. v. DPW*; Act 119 of 2013 official text) — burden/evidence provisions appear in debriefs only from the shared verified set (§ 6341(c.2)(4), (c.2)(5), (c.3), (a)(2), (g)).

Source families involve substance abuse, incarceration, and multi-year histories — none used. The composite's precipitating facts are housing loss and doubling-up (February), narrowed at adjudication to housing/supervision findings; children renamed and re-aged (Maya, 14; Jonah, 8; Wren, 5), mother re-occupationed (school-bus monitor/night cleaner), grandmother invented as a retired school-kitchen worker, county unnamed, all dialogue invented. ### New law verified

**None** — debriefs and the strategy guide use only the shared verified set.

### Withholdings

No § 2511/TPR outcome for any child (the story ends ~day 119 with all tracks open); no day-count for any kinship-approval appeal (none arises — the grandmother passes assessment in-story); the CPSL hearing itself is never depicted, only its owed future (burden, evidence production, 45-day decision clock).
