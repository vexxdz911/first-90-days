# Strategy Guide — Scenario 24: "The Appeal Record"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "Remand — The Argument That Existed."**

State it honestly: this is a **partial** win, and it is the best this scenario offers. The panel vacates the adjudication order and sends the case back for a new hearing on a proper record. Eli does **not** come home at the end of this ending. His placement continues, the case below continues, and the federal fifteen-month date still arrives on schedule — the win is that a documented exception packet is already in the file when it does, and that the new hearing will be fought on a record the parent actually built. There is no ending in this scenario where the appeal itself reunifies the family. Anyone selling you one is selling you a different game.

## (2) The route

Verified by walking the scenario graph from the start node (see the verification note at the end). Key decision points, in order — node title, winning choice, and what it changes:

1. **The Record Arrives in Boxes** — *Order the complete transcripts and request the full agency record, in writing.* Cash −35 (you finish the route with $95 of the $170 start). Sets **recordsRequested** and **transcriptOrdered**; hidden item: complete transcript set ordered, agency record requested. Every later page citation depends on this.
2. **Flashback: The First Hearing** — *Let the hearing move on after grandmother is named on the record.* Sets **kinshipOnRecord**; hidden item: grandmother named as a placement resource in the shelter care transcript. This sentence is what the kinship and exception arguments below are later built from.
3. **Flashback: The Exhibit** — *OBJECT to the scored assessment summary coming in without a witness who can be questioned about it.* Sets **preservedObjection** — the single most important flag in the scenario. It creates a *ruling*, and a ruling is what an appellate panel can review. Without it, the best ending and the split ending both close.
4. **Flashback: The Folder in Your Lap** — *OFFER the folder (treatment certificate, work schedule, pediatrician's letter).* Sets **exhibitInRecord**. The exhibits become numbered, citable pages. Together with the objection, this unlocks the remand ending at the final argument.
5. **Flashback: Your Name Is Called** — *TESTIFY.* Sets **parentTestified**. Your account enters the transcript in your own words; the panel can weigh what the trial court actually had in front of it. (Resting is a principled choice in the fiction — but it leaves the agency's narrative as the transcript's only one.)
6. **Flashback: Disposition and the Plan** — *OBJECT and ask, on the record, for grandmother's assessment to be ordered and the plan corrected.* Sets **preservedDispo**. A ruling instead of a hallway promise — reachable on appeal *and* enforceable below while the appeal runs.
7. **Meanwhile, Below: The Review** — *Hand up the written progress summary with certificates attached.* Cash −10. Sets **reviewRecord**. The case below never pauses for the appeal; this keeps your version in its record too.
8. **The Missing Forty Pages** — *Order the missing transcript pages and renew the written request for the complete agency file.* Cash −20. Sets **recordComplete** (and re-sets **recordsRequested**). Arguing from excerpts around a gap is how the disposition issue falls through the floor.
9. **The Brief Takes Shape** — *Read the brief line by line against the transcripts.* Sets **briefChecked**; two wrong citations get fixed.
10. **The Appendix Fight** — *Check every cited page against the certified record.* Sets **certifiedChecked**. One beloved page turns out to live only in the reproduced record — where, to a panel, it is treated as non-existent — and comes out of the brief before it can sink it.
11. **Visits, in the Present Tense** — *Ask, in writing, for the visit log to note the make-up visit and school award.* Sets **visitLog**. The bottom timeline's record is being written weekly; this writes your week into it.
12. **The Other Clock, Up Close** — *Build and file the written exception packet below before the fifteen-month date.* Cash −10. Sets **compellingPacket**. This is what the remand ending's debrief relies on: when the ASFA date strikes, the agency must reckon with a document, not a recollection.
13. **The Court's Calendar** — *Take the first argument date.* This is a route choice, not a flag: taking the later, easier slot leads directly to the "Still Pending" ending, in which the termination petition is filed while the appeal waits.
14. **Counsel Rises** — *Lead with the preserved objection AND the offered exhibits.* This choice is flag-gated: it is only available with **preservedObjection** + **exhibitInRecord** both set. It leads to **Remand — The Argument That Existed**.

Supporting route choices that cost nothing and keep the folder honest: written schedule requests at **Two Courts, One Work Schedule** (**schedulePaper**), filing the teacher's note with the case below at **The School Report, Meanwhile**, and pressing for a dated answer on the exception at **Meanwhile, Below: Another Review**.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **The appeal runs on the record, and the record is built at the trial level.** The route is nothing but record-building at the moments the law provides for it: a hearing held promptly (shelter care within 72 hours — 42 Pa.C.S. § 6332; 23 Pa.C.S. § 6315(d)), with counsel (right to counsel/appointed counsel — 42 Pa.C.S. § 6337), at which objections create rulings and offered exhibits become evidence. Stated generically in the scenario, and deliberately without appellate-rule citations: a panel reviews what was preserved below, and unpreserved issues are generally not reached.
- **Agency records are obtainable on request** — 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d) ("shall be released upon request"). Steps 1 and 8 are that right, exercised in writing, twice.
- **The case below does not pause, and its federal clock has documented exceptions.** At fifteen of the most recent twenty-two months, the filing duty changes *unless* an exception is documented: relative placement, a documented compelling reason, or services the agency was required to provide but did not (42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E)). Step 12 is that "documented" requirement taken literally. A termination petition, if filed, would still require the agency to prove its grounds by **clear and convincing** evidence — the route never treats the petition as the ending.
- The remand itself is the system working as designed: an error preserved on a proper record is corrected by doing the hearing again, correctly. Process, not rescue.

## (4) The traps

1. **Staying silent at "Flashback: The Exhibit"** because objecting feels like making a scene. Cost: no **preservedObjection** — both the remand and split endings close, and the only leads left at argument are the ones that end in affirmance. The packet is admitted "like weather," and no ruling ever exists to appeal.
2. **Holding the folder at "Flashback: The Folder in Your Lap"** because the judge seems impatient. Cost: no **exhibitInRecord**. The pediatrician's letter stays real and unusable forever — the panel cannot review a document that was never offered, and mailing it later does not put it in the record.
3. **Working from the agency's excerpts at "The Record Arrives in Boxes"** to save $35. Cost: no complete transcripts, no **recordComplete** when the forty pages go missing — the brief ends up arguing a disposition issue from memory, in a forum that does not run on memory.
4. **Taking the later argument slot at "The Court's Calendar"** because work is calmer then. Cost: the whole appeal. The ASFA date strikes first, the petition is filed from whatever the file holds, and the scenario ends with the appeal still pending — preserved record intact, and suddenly beside the point.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The panel's reading of the ruling you preserved. The trial judge's original decision to admit the summary anyway. Both courts' calendars, the agency's filing choices, your employer's patience, and the plain luck of which pages a transcript service loses. The route above does not control any of that — it only guarantees that when the uncontrollable happens, there is a record for it to happen *to*. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, judges, and luck as much as on any parent's choices. The strategy's whole claim is narrower: the parts that *were* yours — a spoken objection, an offered folder, a filed packet — were done in writing, in the room, on time.
