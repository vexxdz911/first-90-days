# STRATEGY GUIDE — Scenario 14: "The Waiting List" (Hard)

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation. This guide describes strategy inside the scenario only. It is general information about how the simulated system works — not legal advice for any real case.*

**Path check:** The route below was verified by walking `scenario-14.json` programmatically — every choice named here exists at the named node, and the sequence reaches the named ending. End cash on this route: $38 of $140 (the bus pass is the big spend).

## 1. The win

**"Ending — The Delay Has a Name."** Be precise about what this is: a *documented exception*, not a finished case. The six-week delay is attributed on the record to the program's waitlist; the report's implication that you "began late" does not survive its own attachments. But Harborview still has nine weeks to run, Wren is still placed, and no debrief in this scenario calls the case won. The win is that the system's delay is recorded as the system's — permanently, for every future reader of the file — which is the record the federal filing clock makes valuable (see §3).

4. **Two Buses** — Choose *"Spend the pass money and protect the routes."* (cash −$96) Changes: keeps every controllable line clean — visits, screens, meetings — so that at the review, the *only* outstanding line is the one the program owns.

## 3. Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

- **The ASFA filing clock and its services exception.** Under 42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E), when a child has been in foster care for 15 of the most recent 22 months, the agency is generally to file for termination — **except**, among the statute's three exceptions, where the agency has identified services necessary for the child's safe return that it has not provided to the family. A court-ordered program stuck on a six-week waitlist is that exception's exact shape. But the exception is an *argument*, and arguments need exhibits: the record must show the service was identified and not provided in time — not offered and declined. The folder is what makes it provable.
- **The record is available on written request** (55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)). The request is what surfaces the chronology's "Parent to begin" — the sentence the whole folder exists to answer.
- **Counsel assembles, attaches, and files** (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337) — the scenario's thesis in the lawyer's words: "If the report says you delayed, we don't argue. We attach."
- **The exception's honest limits.** It speaks to the *filing decision*. Any later termination case would still have to be proven by the agency by clear and convincing evidence, on grounds this scenario never reaches. The exception buys the truth a hearing; it does not decide the hearing.

## 4. The traps

- **Taking the verbal estimate at intake** (*Name on the List*, second choice). The start date lives in your memory — accurate, sincere, and inadmissible. Every later document is harder to build without the anchor page.
- **Managing the wait by phone** (*The Thursday Call*, second choice → "Warm and Unrecorded"). The coordinator remembers you; the file doesn't. Six weeks of diligent contact prove exactly as much as her memory of it.
- **Raising the triage week at the front desk** (*A Slot Opens*, second choice → "The Front Desk"). Warm, sympathetic, unrecorded. The "opening the parent did not take" sentence stays available for a report writer to use.
- **Answering the draft report from memory** (*The Review*, third choice). Your voice is believed the way voices are believed in rooms that run on attachments — personally, warmly, and without effect: **"Ending — Six Weeks, Rewritten."** Presenting partial paper reaches the middle ending, "Started Late, Half-Credited" — the judge contests the delay rather than attributing it, which is what contested paper earns.

## 5. What no strategy controls

A different judge could note the attribution and press the timeline anyway — Wren's clock is real too; she spent those six weeks four years old, whatever the paperwork says. The program might never have put "capacity" in writing; a report writer might contest the folder; a coordinator who doesn't answer email breaks the Thursday rhythm (paper letters would have to replace it). Workers, clinicians, judges, and triage arithmetic all hold pieces of this outcome. What the route controls is narrower: whether a delay everyone agreed was not your fault is *documented* as not your fault — because in this system, undocumented innocence expires.
