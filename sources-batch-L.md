# SOURCES AND ALTERATIONS — Batch L: Scenarios 97–106 (Special Circumstances)

*How "The First 90 Days" Scenarios 97–106 were built from the published public record and the project's verified research, what was drawn from each source, what was changed, and the date arithmetic for every clock.*

**Status:** Draft documentation, for internal development and attorney review. Nothing here or in the scenarios is published or sent.

**Source rule applied:** Every scenario is a composite built from published Pennsylvania Superior Court, Commonwealth Court, and Supreme Court opinions in dependency and termination-of-parental-rights matters, from official statutory text, and from documented system patterns across many cases. No private case, file, docket, or person from the development team's own orbit was used as source material, in any form. The named private cases in the owner's orbit were excluded absolutely.

**Alteration doctrine applied:** Procedural spines and testimony-textured beats come from the published sources and documented patterns; names, ages, counties, occupations, programs, and identifying facts are changed, and beats from at least two sources or patterns are merged in every scenario, so no real family maps onto any character. All ten families are invented composites. In this batch the antagonist is always a documented system failure mode — paper that never travels, services delivered in a form the parent cannot use, a victim scored for someone else's conduct, poverty items recorded as risk — never a parent's identity and never an individual worker written as a villain.

**Engine-flag note (all ten scenarios):** `recordsRequested` is set on every winning route — in this batch, as in Batch J, the record request is the strategy's spine, and the coordinator's validation required a gated, cash-safe path to each best ending on which the flag is set. The `removed` flag follows each story's facts: S97 has no removal (a kinship safety arrangement); S104 follows Batch J discipline (placement predates the playable permanency year, flag deliberately absent, no shelter-care banner); the remaining scenarios set it only where a removal or placement occurs inside the playable story, with the shelter care hearing landing within 72 hours in every such branch.

**New law verified in this batch: NONE.** Every scenario uses only the shared verified set: shelter care within 72 hours (42 Pa.C.S. § 6332; 23 Pa.C.S. § 6315(d)); counsel (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337; § 6332(a)); indicated-report appeal window (23 Pa.C.S. § 6341(a)(2)) incl. Act 119 where invoked; § 6341(a)/(c)/(d); disposition and placement review (42 Pa.C.S. § 6351(a)/(b)/(e)(3)(i)/(f), incl. (f)(9) and (f.1)); notice (42 Pa.C.S. § 6336.1, Act 48 of 2023) and kinship definitions (23 Pa.C.S. § 6302); ASFA 15 of the most recent 22 months and its three exceptions (42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E)); the clear-and-convincing burden, referenced generically and only at the filing stage; records released on request (55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)); § 3130.44(f) redaction limits; Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340 where invoked, with its Universal Assessment application flagged untested. **No ICWA, ADA, immigration, or military-specific law (including the SCRA) is asserted anywhere in the batch** — see Withholdings. No assessment is called AI, machine learning, or predictive anywhere in the batch.

## Published sources and documented patterns used across the batch

1. **In re K.M., 2023 PA Super 217** — referrals that were "empty actions," paper lost at caseworker handoffs. The batch's most-used texture: institutional paper that never reaches the agency file (S97, S104), the counselor letter "in the system" but unfindable (S100), the covering-worker summary "generally compliant, details unclear" (S101), the voicemail-box referral (S103), the covering worker's re-blended draft (S106).
2. **In re T.J.J.M. (Pa. Super. 2018)** (summary level) — a visit offered once, canceled, never rescheduled; services not fitted to the parent's life. Direct ancestor of S105 (the batch's services-fit scenario); texture also in S97, S100, S101, S104 (visits and plan services scheduled at times and in forms the facility or the parent could not honor). Its reasonable-efforts framing was **not** used as law (see Withholdings).
3. **In re J.L.L.** — case-plan objective sets, progress-rating vocabulary ("no progress noted"), pay-stub proof, visit-log texture. S97, S101, S102, S103, S105, S106.
4. **In re A.L.K.** — the child's own timeline as counterweight, present in debriefs throughout; in S101 doubled (Desiree is both the child of one file and the parent of the other); in S106 carried by a teenager's own arithmetic of the days.
5. **In re A.P.C.-D.** — what the certified record actually supports; the discipline behind every written-correction beat (S102, S105, S106) and the record-first structure of the batch generally.
6. **In re T.D., 949 A.2d 910 (Pa. Super. 2008)** — the permanency-review framework in which the 15-of-22 determination is made on the record at review. S102, S104, S106.
7. **In re N.R.A.** — housing-waitlist and timeline texture at the permanency point. S102, S103.
8. **In re K.C., Jr.** and **In re J.W.** — premature goal change reversed on present compliance, and its counterweight. Texture for the goal-drift and goal-change endings (S101, S104, S105), narrative framing only.
9. **Documented pattern (unlabeled, across many published opinions): "failure to protect."** Victim-parents scored for a partner's violence; plan conditions drafted with the victim as sole subject, requiring her to control an adult she cannot control; the partner's conduct rendered as background weather. The controlling pattern for S99 and S106.
10. **Documented pattern: poverty recorded as neglect.** Assessment items that measure price rather than danger — no kitchen, utilities in another name, food insecurity, motel living — entered as risk factors. The controlling pattern for S103.
11. **Documented pattern: the accommodation that was never offered.** Plans, classes, and evaluations delivered in formats the parent cannot use (dense written English handed to a Deaf mother for signature; a four-hour evaluation battery spanning a brain-injured father's documented fade; a 6 p.m. class against his fatigue curve), then scored as the parent's non-cooperation or failure. The controlling pattern for S98 and S105.
12. **Documented pattern: treatment scored as use.** A prescribed medication line in a hospital chart read as the story of a mother in medication-assisted treatment; her year of screens and attendance filed in a different building from the file that scores her; hospital social-work reports at delivery opening cases on newborns. The controlling pattern for S100.
13. **Documented pattern: separation by default paperwork.** Teen parents in care parted from their babies not by any finding but because no single form asks the joint question; mother-baby placements that exist but must be asked for in writing. The controlling pattern for S101.
14. **Documented pattern: the unreachable parent.** Deployed parents' cases proceeding on shore-side paperwork — mail to a vacated address scored as "no response," a "non-cooperation" record built from silence the file itself manufactured. The controlling pattern for S102.
15. **Documented pattern: progress in the other filing cabinet.** Incarcerated parents' institutional records (classes, work assignments, clear conduct) that never reach the agency file; case plans mailed to facilities and returned unclaimed. The controlling pattern for S97 and S104.

## Withholdings

- **Disability law (ADA and any state analogue)** — not asserted or cited in S98 or S105, although both stories turn on accommodations never offered. The wins rest on documentation discipline and the verified dependency mechanics; both strategy guides state honestly that disability-specific protections exist beyond the verified set and were not relied on. No interpreter *entitlement* is ever stated in S98 — only that the mother asked in writing and kept the paper.
- **Military law (SCRA and any service-member protection)** — not asserted or cited in S102. The best ending's debrief and the strategy guide state plainly that such protections exist in the wider law but are outside the verified set and unrelied-upon; every winning move is a generic documented move available to any parent.
- **Protection-from-abuse and criminal-procedure law** — not cited in S99 or S106. The stay-away order is generic process description only ("a judge's order that he stay away"); no conditions, durations, or procedures are invented, and the criminal case's continuances are described as the other court's ordinary weather.
- **Drug-testing, hospital-reporting, and "plan of safe care" rules** — none asserted in S100; hospital and treatment beats are generic process description with no citation, no invented rule, and no invented deadline.
- **Minors' consent, emancipation, and foster-youth parenting programs** — no law asserted in S101; the joint placement is pursued purely as documented process (written requests in both files, record requests, corrections, § 6351 reviews).
- **Prison-specific rights and release/parole rules** — none asserted in S97 or S104; mail, calls, video visits, and the release date are generic process facts of the fiction, and S104 describes release only as possible after the fifteen-month date.
- **Benefits, housing-program, and waitlist rules** — none stated as law in S103; amounts, eligibility, and program mechanics are generic process texture only.
- **23 Pa.C.S. § 2511 grounds and § 2513 procedure** — not used anywhere in the batch. S104 and S106 end at the filing decision, as Batches E and J did; where a petition is filed in a lesser ending, the debrief states expressly that a petition is not a termination and that the burden would be clear and convincing.
- **Reasonable efforts as an element of termination grounds** — not asserted anywhere, for the reason recorded in Batch J's withholdings; the services point appears only inside the § 675(5)(E) filing exception and the § 6351 record, exactly as the verified set allows.
