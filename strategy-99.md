# Strategy Guide — Scenario 99: "His Name in the Right Column"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "His Name in the Right Column."**

State it honestly: this is a **partial** win, and it is the best this scenario offers. Hazel and Olive stay home — and were home for nearly all of the fifty-three days the story covers — and the family service plan is rewritten so its conditions name the actual risk (the father's conduct, in the risk column, by name) and the mother's actual actions (notify, document, maintain the kin plan, keep the stay-away order current). The assessment narrative in the file is corrected to match. But the case is **still open**, a review is still coming, and every future incident will be read against this file. The win is that it will be read against a corrected file. There is no ending in this scenario where the case closes or the night that opened it is undone. Anyone selling you one is selling you a different game.

## (2) The route

Verified by walking the scenario graph from the start node (see the verification note at the end). Key decision points, in order — node title, winning choice, and what it changes:

1. **The Notebook, Page One** — *Start the notebook: dates, times, your own actions, in ink.* Sets **protectiveLog** — the single most important flag in the scenario. A dated record of the mother's own protective actions, begun on day one, before anyone asked for it.
2. **Mr. Albright at Four** — *Show him the notebook and name Lucinda as your call, in front of the form.* Hidden item: the home as observed, with the log and the sister named as support in the interview itself. What the worker sees is what the scored assessment form can record.
3. **The Plan, First Draft** — *Take the plan to Ms. Radcliffe and read every condition with her before signing.* Cash −10 (bus fare). Sets **counselRead**. This is where the plan's verbs get read — ensure, maintain, prevent, every one of them assigned to the mother — and where the strategy of the whole scenario is named.
4. **Lucinda, in Writing** — *Ask Lucinda to put it on one signed page: availability, address, "call me first."* Sets **lucindaWritten**. Kin support becomes a document the file has to carry, and one of the three flags the final gate requires.
5. **The Paper She Sought** — *File the stamped copies of the stay-away paperwork with the agency, in writing.* Sets **stayAwayFiled**. The mother's own protective filing becomes part of the record the assessment should have been reading all along.
6. **The Neighbor's Sentence** — *Accept Mrs. Osei's written statement and add it to the folder.* Sets **neighborWritten**. Context — the mother observed calling for help during the incident — enters the file in signed form.
7. **The Request Itself** — *Sign and send the written record request today.* Sets **recordsRequested**. The agency record, including the scored assessment form, is requested in writing under 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d) — early, while its characterizations are still correctable lines in a file.
8. **One Page, With Attachments** — *File the one-page correction with every attachment, in writing, copies kept.* Sets **correctionFiled** — the second flag the final gate requires. The correction separates his conduct (the risk) from her actions (the record) and asks that the narrative and the plan be rewritten to match.
9. **Copies, Postage, Bus Fare** — *Pay for the copies and postage — the folder goes everywhere, complete.* Cash −20. A correction with its attachments is an exhibit; without them it is a claim.
10. **The Second Visit** — *Walk him through the folder, page by page, before he leaves.* Hidden item: kin support present and documented; the worker's own note begins "Mother has documented." Sent paper and seen paper are cousins, not twins — this makes them twins.
11. **The Call You Were Told to Make** — *Report the attempted contact the same day, in writing as well as by phone, with the notebook page attached.* Hidden item: the self-report, documented. This is the beat the losing endings turn on: the identical call, landing in an uncorrected file, is read as evidence about her instead of about him.
12. **Naming the Actual Risk** — *Leave the written plan-revision ask with the supervisor, dated, and keep the stamped copy.* Cash −10. Sets **rewriteAsked**. The decision is promised in writing — and a written decision can be answered, built on, or challenged; a spoken one can only be remembered differently.
13. **Wednesday, Again** — *Stand on the written record: "Everything I was asked to do is in that file, dated — judge the file."* This choice is flag-gated: it is only available with **protectiveLog** + **correctionFiled** + **lucindaWritten** all set. It leads to the written decision rewriting the plan.
14. **The Decision, in Writing** — *Take the written decision home and put your copy in the folder, on top.* The route terminates at **His Name in the Right Column**.

Supporting route choices that cost nothing and keep the folder honest: filing Hazel's school note at **Hazel's Reading Level** (ordinary life, documented, beside the worst night), and asking at **The Meeting With a Supervisor** that the plan decision issue in writing either way.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **Agency records are obtainable on request** — 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d) ("shall be released upon request"). Step 7 is that right, exercised in writing at day 17 rather than discovered at a hearing. It is what converts the scored assessment form from weather into information: the mother sees her name in the risk column while the narrative can still be corrected, and her filed correction (step 8) goes into the same file the form came out of.
- **Parents in these proceedings are represented** — the right to counsel, and to appointed counsel if a parent cannot afford one, is stated in the removal branch from 42 Pa.C.S. § 6337. Ms. Radcliffe's function in the winning route is the function counsel actually performs in the documented pattern this scenario is built from: reading the operative document's verbs, and moving the argument from the mother's character to the plan's drafting.
- **When a removal does occur, the clock is hours, not weeks** — an informal shelter care hearing not later than 72 hours after placement (42 Pa.C.S. § 6332; 23 Pa.C.S. § 6315(d)). The removal ending is built on exactly that clock (placed Wednesday, December 17, 2025; heard Friday, December 19, 2025), which is why the record has to be built *before* any incident forces the question.
- **Review and disposition run on the plan and record that exist** (42 Pa.C.S. § 6351, as used in prior batches). The unrevised-plan ending is that principle experienced from inside: a court applies the document in front of it. The winning route does not argue that the mother is good; it gets the document rewritten — conditions stated as actions within her power, the source of risk named in the document's own words — *before* an adjudication applies the old wording to a new incident. That is a drafting victory, and the scenario is honest that drafting is what was won.

## (4) The traps

1. **Trusting memory at "The Notebook, Page One"** because you were there and will remember clearly. Cost: no **protectiveLog** — the final stand at "Wednesday, Again" is unavailable, because there is no dated day-one record to stand on. True memories describe; dated pages prove, and the hearing runs on the second.
2. **Keeping Lucinda verbal at "Lucinda, in Writing"** because her word has been good for thirty years. Cost: no **lucindaWritten** — the third gate flag. However willing Lucinda is in life, the court meets her as a summary, and summaries do not hold a placement plan together under pressure.
3. **Raising the correction by phone at "One Page, With Attachments"** because paper feels confrontational and Mr. Albright seemed fair. Cost: no **correctionFiled**. When the second incident is self-reported, the file's newest characterization is still the form's — "exposure," with her name in the risk column — and the route terminates at **Removed After the Call She Made Herself**: placement, a shelter care hearing within 72 hours, and the same pages to build afterward at ten times the price.
4. **Judging the plan as written at "Wednesday, Again"** — accepting condition-by-condition application of a document whose conditions require the mother to control another adult. Cost: **The Plan as Written**. The girls come home under the original conditions, word for word, and every future incident — including ones she reports, as the plan commands — is scored against wording she cannot by its own construction satisfy.

## (5) What no strategy controls

His choices — whether he comes back, and when. The neighbor's original call, the supervisor assigned to the meeting, the caseworker's rotation, or whether the next incident happens at all. The route above controls none of that. It controls only the state of the file when the uncontrollable arrives: whether the call a mother was ordered to make is read as compliance or as exposure depends, in this scenario's documented pattern, on what else is in the folder that day. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, judges, and luck as much as on any parent's choices. The strategy's whole claim is narrower: the parts that were hers — a notebook started on day one, a sister's signature, a filing she sought herself, a correction sent in writing — were done early, on paper, where the scoring happens.
