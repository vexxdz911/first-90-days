# SOURCES AND ALTERATIONS — Batch J: Scenarios 77–86 (Permanency Clocks)

*How "The First 90 Days" Scenarios 77–86 were built from the published public record and the project's verified research, what was drawn from each source, what was changed, and the date arithmetic for every clock.*

**Status:** Draft documentation, for internal development and attorney review. Nothing here or in the scenarios is published or sent.

**Source rule applied:** Every scenario is a composite built from published Pennsylvania Superior Court, Commonwealth Court, and Supreme Court opinions in dependency and termination-of-parental-rights matters, from official statutory text, and from documented system patterns across many cases. No private case, file, docket, or person from the development team's own orbit was used as source material, in any form. The named private cases in the owner's orbit were excluded absolutely.

**Alteration doctrine applied:** Procedural spines and testimony-textured beats come from the published sources; names, ages, counties, occupations, programs, and identifying facts are changed, and beats from at least two published sources are merged in every scenario, so no real family maps onto any character. All ten families are invented composites.

**Engine-flag note (all ten scenarios):** `recordsRequested` is set on every winning route — in this batch the record request is not a subplot, it is the strategy. The `removed` flag is deliberately **absent** from all ten: each story opens with the child already in foster care and plays the permanency year; no removal occurs inside the playable story, so the engine's 72-hour shelter-care banner must not fire.

**New law verified in this batch: NONE.** Every scenario uses only the shared verified set: ASFA 15 of the most recent 22 months and its three exceptions (42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E)); records released on request (55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)); counsel (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337); the clear-and-convincing burden for termination grounds, referenced generically and only at the filing stage. **No § 2511 grounds are analysed, no termination outcome is depicted or predicted, and no goal-change or concurrent-planning proposition is stated as primary-text law** — those beats are narrative framing drawn from the opinions below, exactly as Batch E handled Scenario 33. No assessment is called AI, machine learning, or predictive anywhere in the batch.

## Published sources used across the batch (pattern sources)

1. **In the Interest of J.L.L., No. 1714 EDA 2023 (Pa. Super. Apr. 26, 2024)** — case-plan objective sets, progress-rating vocabulary, pay-stub proof, visit-log texture. Principal texture for S77, S81, S84.
2. **In re Adoption of N.R.A., No. 915 WDA 2025 (Pa. Super. 2026)** — housing-waitlist testimony at the permanency point; compliance arcs measured against the filing date. S77, S84.
3. **In re T.J.J.M. (Pa. Super. 2018)** (summary level) — a visit offered once, canceled, never rescheduled; services not fitted to the parent's life. Used as texture only, exactly as in Scenario 3: the canceled-visit and services-gap beats in S78, S85. Its reasonable-efforts framing was **not** used as law (see Withholdings).
4. **In re Adoption of A.P.C.-D., No. 1432 MDA 2025 (Pa. Super. 2026)** — appellate parsing of what the certified record actually supports; the discipline behind S82's draft-answered-from-the-record structure.
5. **In re A.L.K. (Pa. Super. 2016)** — the child's own timeline as counterweight ("a child's life cannot be held in abeyance"), present in debriefs so the exceptions are taught against the child's clock, not around it. All scenarios.
6. **In re T.D., 949 A.2d 910 (Pa. Super. 2008)** — the permanency-review framework in which the 15-of-22 determination is made on the record at review. S81, S86.
7. **In re R.J.T., 9 A.3d 1179 (Pa. 2010)**, quoting **In re Adoption of S.E.G., 901 A.2d 1017 (Pa. 2006)** — concurrent planning as a dual-track system; goal change denied where reunification remained possible on an actively-pursued plan. Narrative framing for S80, S86 (no new primary-text proposition asserted).
8. **In the Interest of K.C., Jr., No. 667 MDA 2021 (Pa. Super. Nov. 19, 2021)** — goal change reversed as premature where the parent was attending, screening, and completing intake on a briefly-served plan. S80.
9. **In the Interest of J.W., No. 235 WDA 2020 (Pa. Super. Oct. 19, 2020)** — the counterweight: goal change affirmed on moderate-to-minimal compliance. The honest other column in S80, S86.
10. **In the Interest of K.M., 2023 PA Super 217** — referrals that were "empty actions," lost at caseworker handoffs, documented from the agency's own record. The services-exception texture for S78, S85, and S83's plan-coordination beats.

## Withholdings

- **Reasonable efforts as an element of termination grounds** — not asserted anywhere (the D.C.D./T.J.J.M. line was rejected by the Supreme Court, as recounted in In re T.R.C.). The services point appears only inside the § 675(5)(E) filing exception, exactly as the verified set allows.
- **23 Pa.C.S. § 2511 grounds and § 2513 procedure** — not used. The batch ends at the filing decision, as Scenarios 3, 24, and 33 did.
- **Any appeal day-count for goal-change orders** — none stated; no primary text for one was read in this round.
- **Universal Assessment / Rule 1340** — not invoked in this batch; the permanency record, not the assessment, is the subject. Where hidden items accumulate, they are described as the case file's quiet record, never as a score produced by software of any claimed kind.
