# Strategy Guide — Scenario 3: “Fifteen Months”

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation. “In this scenario” means in the scenario’s data and debriefs. This is general information, not legal advice for any real case.*

## 1. The win

Scenario 3 has three winning endings. Ranked by outcome:

1. **“Ending — Home at Month Sixteen”** — the strongest ending. Noah comes home under the court’s order at month 16. This is the only full reunification ending.
2. **“Ending — Documented”** — a major legal win, but partial: an ASFA exception is documented and the termination petition is deferred. Noah remains placed and the case continues. The services-gap version is the strongest *controllable* strategy because the agency confronts its own unsent referral and does not contest the dates.
3. **“Ending — Family, While You Finish”** — also a real win, but more partial: Noah moves to his aunt Grace under the relative-care exception while reunification remains the goal and the mother finishes housing.

**“Ending — The Petition Is Filed”** is not a win at this stage, although the scenario correctly stresses that a petition is not itself a termination.

The route below reaches the strongest ending. Its final housing step depends on the waitlist moving; the strategy’s job is to make the file ready if that break arrives.

## 3. Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

- **ASFA creates a filing duty, not an automatic termination.** At 15 of the most recent 22 months, **42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E)** requires the agency to file or join a termination petition unless an exception applies. The scenario is built around the word “unless.”
- **The three exceptions must be made true in the record.** The statute’s doors, as the scenario states them, are: the child is being cared for by a relative; the agency has documented a compelling reason in the case plan why filing would not be in the child’s best interest; or the agency has not provided services identified as necessary for the child’s safe return. Oral progress and sympathetic facts are not substitutes for documentation.
- **The record request is the reconnaissance that makes strategy possible.** Under **55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)**, the case record “shall be released upon request.” The renewed request reveals the current ratings, the cancellation pattern, and the unsent referral before the June review.
- **Paper converts effort into facts a court can use.** A dated waitlist letter proves position 214. A director’s letter proves both achievement and the residence rule. Pay stubs prove the employment year. A written make-up request proves that the parent did not simply vanish from canceled visits.
- **The services exception is about the plan the agency itself wrote.** The family-housing program was identified as necessary, marked for referral, and never sent. The winning services route does not accuse the worker of malice; it establishes dates and asks the system to honor the distinction its own file created.
- **A petition would still not be a termination.** The scenario states that the agency would have to prove statutory grounds by clear and convincing evidence at a later hearing. The exceptions matter because they prevent that separate fight from being scheduled on an incomplete record.

## 4. The traps

- **Taking the housing position verbally** leaves “around 214” as a rumor. Without the letter, the compelling-reason packet loses one of its hardest facts.
- **Absorbing the third cancellation without a written make-up request** lets the log show another blank while recording no parental request and no agency follow-through problem.
- **Letting the renewed record request ride** is the information trap. The unsent referral remains in the file, but neither you nor your lawyer builds around it before June.
- **Keeping your head down because “substantial” should speak for itself** reaches **“Ending — The Petition Is Filed.”** The rating proves progress; it does not document any ASFA exception.
- **Accepting documented deferral when your target is reunification** is a lesser-win trap only in ranking, not a failure: it closes the month-16 home ending for this route while preserving the case and buying time in writing.

## 5. What no strategy controls

The waitlist moves at its own speed. Foster-family emergencies cancel visits. Grace’s study can be delayed. The agency’s lawyer and the judge still decide what to do with the record placed before them. The scenario’s honest center is that documentation and dates—not effort alone—are what the clock meets in June. The strongest route therefore does two things: it builds every controllable fact early, and it leaves the case positioned to benefit if housing luck finally arrives.
