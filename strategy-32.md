## (1) The win

**“Ending — Two Ratings, Revised in Writing.”**

Read this plainly, because this scenario measures its win by what got onto the record, and nothing else: Eli does **not** come home at the review hearing. The form itself is never produced — you finish the scenario having never seen it. What the winning route achieves is this: your item-by-item rebuttal worksheet is entered as your exhibit; two of the seventeen Priority Item ratings — supervision and housing stability — are revised downward in a signed agency letter that cites *your own documents* as the basis; the overall level moves one notch and its recommendation changes with it, toward more time with Eli; and the file now permanently contains your two dated requests, your log, your question about who scored you and with what training or certification, the agency's non-answer to that question, and an honestly-flagged, untested Rule 1340 request for the form. That is the best ending this data contains. It is a paper win with a child's extra Saturday inside it, and this guide will not inflate it into anything larger.

## (2) The route

1. **The Summary, Quoted** → “Ask, before she turns the page, what the summary is based on” → **One Sentence**. *You get the worker's one-sentence answer and — the actual move — you write it down, dated. The direct-to-library route (via The Bus Home) converges here; nothing is lost by it.*
2. **What the Form Is, and Isn't** → write the request this week. *The clinic scene gives you the honest facts the whole route stands on: a Pennsylvania-specific scored form in the FAST family, 17 Priority Items, item list and scoring rules never published, a person scores it.*
3. **Request Number One** → “Mail it certified and log it the same day” (cash −6). *Sets FLAG: `recordsRequested` and FLAG: `logStarted`; HIDDEN + the dated request naming the form, all 17 item ratings, and the outcome.*
4. **The Waiting Weeks** → “Start a documents folder.” *Sets FLAG: `folderStarted`. (The Saturday-visit choice also reaches the folder, one scene later — the folder, not the timing of one scene, is what matters.)*
5. **The Folder** → check the mailbox. *HIDDEN + the dated document set — pay stubs, attendance sheet, lease, supervisor letter. This folder is the raw material of every later row.*
6. **The Outcome Summary** → “Write request number two — more specific than the first.” *Accepting the summary here is trap 1: it closes all three record endings and leaves only the unseen ending reachable in full.*
7. **Request Number Two** → mail it certified and log it (cash −6). *Sets FLAG: `secondRequest`; HIDDEN + the narrower request — ratings by item, the form, the rater's role — citing the first request and its reply by date.*
8. **Nine of Seventeen** → begin the worksheet. *Sets FLAG: `ratingsPartial`; HIDDEN + the partial production (9 of 17 ratings; form withheld). Partial answers are this route's fuel: nine specific ratings are nine checkable sentences.*
9. **The Worksheet** → “Finish all nine rows — including the ones that say 'true'.” *Sets FLAG: `worksheetBuilt`; HIDDEN + the full worksheet. Conceding the true rows is deliberate: it is what makes the two disputed rows credible. The two-rows-only version still wins, but thinner.*
10. **The Landlord's Letter** → “Ask her today” (cash −8). *HIDDEN + the landlord letter, attached to the housing row. This single page becomes one of the two documents the agency later cites when revising a rating.*
11. **Who Scored You?** → “Send the rater question in writing, by itself, logged.” *Sets FLAG: `raterAsked`; HIDDEN + the dated question (role, training, certification). Its answer matters less than its existence on the record — see the next steps.*
12. **The Second Team Meeting** → wait for the answer. *HIDDEN + the meeting notes recording, in the agency's own file, that you hold the ratings and a worksheet. Your challenge now exists in their handwriting too.*
13. **The Answer That Isn't** → take the folder to the lawyer. *HIDDEN + the letterhead non-answer (no name, no certification, no date). Filed in order — question, answer, gap — it becomes an exhibit rather than a frustration.*
14. **The Court Track, Honestly** → “Add the Rule 1340 request for the form to the hearing filing — with the untested flag stated in it.” *Sets FLAG: `rule1340Asked`. (Skipping it, via Carrying It In Yourself, still reaches the win — the worksheet does the winning — but spends nothing on the question that may matter in a later case.)*
15. **Filed Before the Hearing** → go to the hearing (cash −40, an unpaid morning). *Worksheet, requests, replies, rater question and non-answer, and the Rule 1340 request are filed and served in advance — paper the other side has seen cannot be kept out of the room.*
16. **The Review Hearing** → “Press the two documented items — walk the judge row by row through the paper.” *This is the fork among the three record endings: pressing the items leads here; offering the worksheet and questions without pressing leads to the long-win ending; asking for attachment and a calendared revisit leads to the middle ending.*
17. **Row by Row** → wait the fourteen days. *The judge sets a written, dated re-scoring obligation; the worksheet stays in the record either way.*
18. **The Letter With Two Corrections** → file the letter and go tell Eli about Saturday. *Reaches the ending.*

Cash along the route: $150 → $90 (−6 and −6 certified mailings, −8 for the landlord trip, −40 unpaid morning for the hearing). No `removed` flag is set anywhere in this scenario, correctly: Eli's placement predates the story by four months, so there is no in-story removal and no 72-hour clock to trigger.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

- **One verified right, exercised specifically.** Under 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d), information contained in case records “shall be released upon request” to a parent. The route is that sentence, operationalized: the request names a specific record — the full scored assessment: the form, the item ratings, and the outcome — and is repeated, narrower, when the first answer returns only a summary. Specificity is what converts a general right into itemized, checkable paper.
- **The UA honesty rules are load-bearing, not decorative.** The Universal Assessment, as the project's research documents it, is a Pennsylvania-specific scored form in the FAST family with 17 Priority Items whose ratings drive decisions; its item list and scoring rules have never been published. It is a scored form — never, in this scenario or this guide, called AI, machine learning, predictive analytics, or an algorithm. That restraint is strategic as well as accurate: the challenge works *because* a person scored it, and a person's scoring can be met with documents, item by item.
- **The untested question, asked honestly.** Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(B)(1) provides for disclosure, on a party's request, of material items in dependency proceedings. Whether that rule reaches the assessment form is untested — no court has yet decided, and the route's filing says so in its own text. Asking a question plainly, flag attached, costs no credibility and puts the question where an answer can someday find it.
- **The record is the remedy the scenario actually promises.** No principle in the verified set entitles a parent to a re-scoring. What the route uses instead is older and simpler: hearings and reviews are decided from files; exhibits, transcripts, and dated letters persist while summaries merely repeat. The worksheet wins revisions not because any rule commands revision, but because a judge, shown documents against ratings, set a clock — the system's ordinary machinery, aimed by paper.

## (4) The traps

1. **Accepting the outcome summary (at The Outcome Summary).** The most reasonable-sounding choice in the scenario, and the most expensive: “the conclusions are what they use anyway” ends the challenge before it exists. From there, only a late, thin version of the middle ending remains reachable — or the unseen ending, in which the summary follows the case unexamined.
2. **Letting it ride (at The Summary, Again).** Attendance feels like evidence, but unrequested documents have a way of not existing. This choice leads straight to the unseen ending: perfect attendance, kindly heard testimony, and a file containing nothing of yours.
3. **Skipping the landlord's letter (at The Landlord's Letter).** The lease proves an address; the form rated a *tenancy*. Without the letter, the housing row is an assertion instead of an exhibit — and housing is one of the two ratings the winning route actually gets revised. The route still reaches the hearing; it arrives with one strong row instead of two.
4. **Doing only the two strong worksheet rows (at The Worksheet).** Tempting triage, and it does not close the win — but a worksheet that never concedes a true item reads as advocacy, and advocacy is discounted. The full nine rows, “true” entries included, are what make the agency's reviewer able to cite your paper without feeling argued with.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The re-scoring letter was written by a person: a different agency reviewer holds both ratings, and nothing in this route can compel otherwise. A different judge declines the fourteen-day clock, or admits the worksheet without acting on it, and the same play produces the long-win ending instead — questions preserved, ratings untouched. The rater question may never be substantively answered in any version of events; the form may never be produced in any of them; eight of the seventeen ratings were never released on any path. Workers rotate, judges differ, reviewers vary, and luck — down to which lawyer staffs a library clinic on a Wednesday — holds real pieces of the outcome. What the route controls is the file: whether it contains, beside the summary, a dated trail of specific asks and a worksheet a future reader can check. In this scenario, that is not a consolation prize. It is the whole game, and the endings are scored accordingly.
