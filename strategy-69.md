# Strategy Guide — Scenario 69: "Answer the Question Asked"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "Not Proven — The Burden Stayed Where It Belongs."**

State it honestly: this is a full win at adjudication, and it is a win on proof, not on praise. The agency does not carry its burden by clear and convincing evidence, the petition is dismissed, and Finn comes home on Friday, November 13, 2026 — with no findings made against Ingrid and no disposition, conditions, or plan, because there is no adjudication to build one on. The scenario's other homecoming ending, **"Dependent, But Home," is a partial win** (labeled partial): one allegation is proven, Finn is adjudicated dependent, and he returns home only under agency supervision and court-prescribed conditions, with the case still open.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **The burden never moves.** At adjudication, the court may find a child dependent only "from clear and convincing evidence" (42 Pa.C.S. § 6341(c)) — and that evidence is the agency's to produce. The parent does not have to prove innocence or fitness to win; the judge decides credibility; a fact not proven is a fact the court cannot use. Every winning step in the route is built to keep the case in that posture: impeachment of the agency's witness, a rebutted summary, testimony that adds no new facts for the other side, and a closing that counts the unproven elements out loud.
- **If the petition is not proven, it is dismissed.** Section 6341(a) is the destination the burden argument is driving at: petition dismissed, child discharged. Because there is no adjudication, there is no disposition at all in the win ending — no conditions, no supervision, no plan.
- **Agency records are obtainable on request** — the record "shall be released upon request" (55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d)). Step 2 is that right, exercised in writing, in time. The impeachment at step 6 is not a courtroom trick; it is what the released record was *for*. A cross-examination cannot invent its material — it can only use what was gathered before the hearing.
- **The scored summary can be answered in writing — as an argument, not a settled right.** Counsel's rebuttal invokes Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(E) (present evidence and argument to rebut a provision) and Rule 1340(F) (factual errors may be challenged). Whether Rule 1340 reaches a scored assessment summary has NOT been tested in a published opinion. The route treats the rebuttal accordingly: it gives the judge a reason to read "unclear" with care; it does not claim the summary was excluded or struck.
- **The partial ending has its own legal shape.** Where one allegation *is* proven by clear and convincing evidence, adjudication follows — and at disposition the court may allow the child to remain with the parent subject to supervision and to conditions the court prescribes (42 Pa.C.S. § 6351(a)(1)). That is "Dependent, But Home": a real exercise of a real disposition option, and still only partial, because the dependency finding itself stands.

## (4) The traps

1. **The volunteered explanation at "Cross-Examination: Answer the Question Asked"** — *"I only left him once, and that was the night the car—"* Cost: the whole adjudication. This is the scenario's named trap, and it is written as love, not foolishness: a mother trying to be fully known. Mechanically, before that sentence the supervision allegation has no direct witness anywhere in the record. After it, the agency no longer has to prove that element — the parent's own testimony supplies it, and the closing on that route can only argue about weight. It leads directly to **"Dependent — Every Word Counted Twice"**: adjudicated substantially on the volunteered admission, placement continuing. The agency's case did not change; the transcript did.
2. **Skipping the written record request at "Ask for the File, in Writing"** because it might antagonize the agency. Cost: the impeachment, and with it the win. Without **recordsRequested**, Petrov's October 14 date stands uncorrected — there is nothing in your side's folder to cross-examine with — and the win-gated closing choice at "Closing: The Burden, or Fairness" never opens. Partial, **"Dependent, But Home,"** is the ceiling on that route.
3. **Skipping witness prep at "The Three Rules of the Witness Chair"** — "I'll just be honest, that's enough." Cost: discipline in the chair. Honesty is not a strategy for cross-examination: without **preppedWitness**, the answer-and-stop choice is unavailable, and the honest-but-rambling testimony at **True, Long, and Everywhere** tangles a plain direct account with weeks nobody asked about, making the home-condition allegation easier for this credibility-watching judge to credit. That route also ends, at best, at **"Dependent, But Home."**
4. **Leaving the scored summary alone at "Supervision: Unclear"** because the Rule 1340 argument is untested. Cost: quiet but real. The summary is the first thing the judge reads; unanswered, its "unclear" stands at full weight. The untested flag is a reason to make the argument carefully, in writing, through counsel — not a reason to let two checkable factual errors sit unrebutted.
5. **Closing on fairness at "Closing: The Burden, or Fairness"** — asking the judge to hear what kind of mother Ingrid is. Cost: the standard. Fairness is not the test at adjudication; the burden is. A fairness closing invites the judge to *feel* something on a record where the winning speech was arithmetic: what was proven, what was corrected, what was never proven at all. On the full winning record it throws away the gate the whole route built; on thinner records it confirms the partial ending.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The judge's own weighing of Petrov's correction — a different judge might shrug at a seven-day date error, and no practice hour can prevent that shrug. The caseworker who wrote a date at the top of his notes at all, and wrote the true one. The teacher's honesty and the school nurse's same-day log, which are luck wearing the clothes of procedure. Whether the recess fall had witnesses. The route above does not control any of that — it only guarantees that when Thursday arrives, the record contains the agency's own notes, an answered summary, and a transcript with no volunteered gifts in it. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, judges, and luck as much as on any parent's choices. The strategy's whole claim is narrower: the burden was the agency's, and on this route nobody carried it for them.
