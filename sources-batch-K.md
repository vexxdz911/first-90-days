# Sources and Alterations — Batch K: Scenarios 87–96, "Appeals & Post-Permanency"

*Wave 2 expansion, produced 2026-10-29 under the owner's doctrine: scenarios built from "likely or documented situations that could happen and how to win the best possible outcome." Documented = published PA Superior/Commonwealth Court opinions and documented system patterns; likely = composites of recurring documented practice. Every family below is a fictional composite. No real person's identifiable case is used; the private cases in the owner's orbit (Christine May, Annie Roth, Camden Ransing) were excluded as sources in full. Drafts only — nothing sent or published; attorney verification remains the gate before any organizational or parent-facing use, as with all Tactic Watch material.*

## How this batch was sourced

This batch's territory — what happens *after* an order: administrative reconsideration, Superior Court appeals, remands, post-permanency reviews, custodianship, and case closure — was built from (a) the statutory and rule text verified for this project (see "New law verified" and the carried-over set below); (b) the documented appellate and post-permanency *patterns* already in the project's sources library from Versions 1–2 and Wave 1 (the appeal-record discipline of Scenario 24; the goal-change framework of *In re R.J.T.* and *In re K.B.* as used in Batch I; the kinship-court texture of *In re D.S.* / *In re J.P.* from Scenario 4; the all-of-the-evidence weighing principle behind *In re Z.P.* / *In re T.S.M.* as used in Batch I's Scenario 75) — used for texture only; and (c) no new opinion research: no scenario in this batch depends on any single opinion's facts, parties, or outcome, and none is reproduced. Every scenario is winnable to its best ending by construction, which the published appellate record — full of affirmances and quashals — is not.

Carried-over verified set (unchanged, worded as in prior batches): 42 Pa.C.S. § 6332 / 23 Pa.C.S. § 6315(d) (72-hour shelter care); § 6337 / § 6332(a) (counsel); 23 Pa.C.S. § 6341(a)(2) (90-day indicated appeal) with Act 119's (c.2)(4), (c.2)(5), (c.3), (g); § 6341(a), (c), (d); § 6351(a)(1), (a)(2)(i), (b), (e)(3)(i), (f) incl. (f)(9)(i)–(iii), (f.1); § 6336.1 (Act 48 of 2023) with § 6302 and § 6357; 42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E) (ASFA 15-of-22 + three exceptions); clear-and-convincing burden; 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d), (f); Pa.R.J.C.P. 1340(A), (B)(1), (E), (F) — Rule 1340's application to a scored assessment remaining expressly UNTESTED wherever it appears (it appears in no Batch K scenario's spine; the batch's record fights are findings-and-exhibits fights, and the sources file says so rather than forcing the beat).

## New law verified this run (primary text read in full)

- **Pa.R.A.P. 903(a)** — "the notice of appeal required by Rule 902 (manner of taking appeal) shall be filed within 30 days after the entry of the order from which the appeal is taken." Read in the official text of the Rules of Appellate Procedure, 210 Pa. Code Ch. 9 (pacodeandbulletin.gov), during this run. This is the clock of Scenarios 88, 93, and 96.
- **Pa.R.A.P. 903(b)** — cross-appeals: "if a timely notice of appeal is filed by a party, any other party may file a notice of appeal within 14 days of the date on which the first notice of appeal was served, or within the time otherwise prescribed by this rule, whichever period last expires." Same official source. This is the clock of Scenario 93 — including its "whichever expires last" structure, which the scenario's day-24 detour relies on expressly and its guide explains.

NOT newly verified, and therefore kept generic in player text (stated as practice's ground rules, never as quoted rules — the same discipline Version 1 applied to appellate mechanics in Scenario 24): preservation and waiver; the certified-record limitation and appendix practice; issue abandonment by failure to press; the concise-statement-of-errors procedure (depicted in Scenario 89 as "a concise statement of the errors," with no rule number cited); supersedeas/stay practice (Scenarios 93/96 state only that an appeal does not by itself pause the case or the parallel adoption track — the formulation prior batches used); the two-question structure of termination appeals (grounds, then needs and welfare) in Scenario 96, stated as the lawyers' framing, with 23 Pa.C.S. § 2511 deliberately NOT cited anywhere in the batch; negotiated contact terms in Scenario 94, taught as mechanism (agreed terms entered in the court's order) with no invented citation.

## Scenario 87 — "The Second Letter" (Foundation, 16 nodes, 2 endings)

**Family:** Sylvie Marchand, a preschool aide; her daughter Fern (5). The Bureau of Hearings and Appeals has upheld her indicated report; the order's last page carries § 6341(g)'s two clocks.
**Documented patterns drawn on:** the BHA track of Scenario 2 continued one step further — reconsideration practice after an adverse final order; material evidence (a pediatric follow-up letter) present in the agency's own (c.2)(4) packet yet never addressed by the final order; registry consequences for childcare employment (the § 6338 texture from Scenario 2's sources).
**Law in the data:** § 6341(g) (15-day reconsideration / 30-day perfection, from the mailing date); § 6341(c.2)(4); § 3130.44(d).
**Alterations:** composite in full; the reconsideration is granted on the unaddressed-evidence ground and orders a new hearing — the ending is labeled partial in its own debrief, and no ending expunges the report.
**Deliberately not used:** no prediction of the new hearing's outcome; no statement that reconsideration tolls the court clock (the scenario teaches the opposite, through the clinic).

## Scenario 88 — "Thirty Days" (Foundation, 18 nodes, 2 endings)

**Family:** Casimir ("Cas") Zielinski, a warehouse/route worker; his son Tomek (6), placed with his aunt after adjudication.
**Documented patterns drawn on:** the 30-day appeal window as the plainest clock in the system (D.S.'s untimely-appeal texture from Scenario 4's sources, restated for a dependency disposition); appointed/private counsel triage by message slip and callback queue; the case-below-continues discipline of Scenario 24, reduced to Foundation scale.
**Law in the data:** Pa.R.A.P. 903(a) (newly verified); § 3130.44(d); § 6337 (counsel, in the debrief).
**Alterations:** composite in full; the lawyer is competent and overloaded, never negligent on the winning route — the loss ending is produced by the parent's delegation, not counsel's failure (contrast S96, where the failure is counsel's and is labeled as such).
**Deliberately not used:** no appellate outcome is depicted or predicted; no filing-fee or docketing specifics beyond the verified clock.

## Scenario 89 — "What the Panel Can See" (Hard, 26 nodes, 3 endings)

**Family:** Rosalind Fenn; her daughter Blythe (9). At permanency review the court changed the goal to adoption by adopting the agency's report "in full," over counsel's preserved objection.
**Documented patterns drawn on:** goal-change review posture (R.J.T./K.B. framework as documented in Batch I); orders that adopt report language without independent findings — the documented failure mode this batch's S76 also used; the appendix/certified-record discipline of Scenario 24.
**Law in the data:** § 6351(f)(2), (3), (5.1), (9); § 6351(f.1); § 6351(b); § 6351(e)(3)(i); 42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E); Pa.R.A.P. 903(a); § 3130.44(d).
**Alterations:** the preserved objection is to the *absence of determinations*, a cleaner and more purely appellate issue than Scenario 24's evidentiary objection — the two scenarios teach different preservation objects on purpose.
**Deliberately not used:** the concise statement of errors is depicted without a rule citation (Pa.R.A.P. 1925 was not re-verified this run); no claim that adoption of a report is always error — the ending's holding, as written, requires the preserved objection plus exhibits answering the report.

## Scenario 92 — "Heard, Not a Party" (Hard, 27 nodes, 3 endings)

**Family:** Philomena Rios (61), kinship caregiver to her granddaughter Solana (8) for eight months; Solana's mother Daniela is the parent in the underlying case.
**Documented patterns drawn on:** § 6336.1 practice as documented in the project's kinship research — notice-and-heard as a real but bounded right; agency preference for fully licensed placements over kinship resources (the pattern SB 1499's findings describe; the bill itself is not mentioned in player text, per Batch 2's instruction); the grandmother-as-recordkeeper of Scenario 4, transposed from approval to permanency.

**Deliberately not used:** no claim that a caregiver may file motions, obtain the record as of right, or appeal — the scenario's clinic scene denies each expressly; no § 3700.72 approval-appeal count (still unverified, as in Scenario 4).

## Scenario 94 — "The Custodianship Question" (Complex, 36 nodes, 4 endings)

**Family:** Thaddeus Cole; his daughter Pearl (4), with her aunt Sabine for 22 of her 48 months. Sabine petitions for permanent legal custodianship; Thaddeus has been stable for eight documented months.
**Documented patterns drawn on:** the (f.1) legal-custodian outcome as the negotiated middle of post-permanency practice; the young-child continuity weighing documented across the published permanency cases in the project's library (stated as pattern, never as rule); "reasonable contact as the parties may agree" as the standard weightless formulation — the scenario's antagonist is a sentence, not a person.
**Law in the data:** § 6351(f); § 6351(f.1); § 3130.44(d). Contact terms are taught as mechanism with no citation, expressly (see "New law" note above).
**Alterations:** the best ending is custodianship WITH the father's negotiated terms — the batch's most deliberate doctrinal choice, argued in the strategy guide rather than assumed: on this record (22 months vs 8, child aged 4), it protects more than the contest's realistic outcomes. The contest remains fully heard and honestly arguable inside the fiction.
**Deliberately not used:** no subsidized-custodianship program specifics, no Orphans' Court adoption procedure, no claim that consent was legally required for the terms — Sabine's agreement is a fact of the fiction, and the guide names the route's dependence on it.

## Scenario 95 — "Earning the Last Order" (Complex, 36 nodes, 3 endings)

**Family:** Imogen Hart; her son Cyril (10), home four months on a § 6351(a)(1) trial basis under conditions and supervision. The April permanency hearing can close the case.
**Documented patterns drawn on:** trial-home/reunification-monitoring practice and discharge at permanency; the file-error pattern (misdated visit, phantom missed appointment) documented across Wave 1; the undisclosed-incident failure mode — a police-attended event surfacing from the report rather than the parent — as the closure posture's characteristic catastrophe.
**Law in the data:** § 6351(a)(1); § 6351(f)(1), (2), (3); § 6351(f.1); § 6351(e)(3)(i); § 3130.44(d). The aftercare plan is presented as agency practice, expressly not as statute.
**Alterations:** composite in full; the doorstep incident involves no allegation against the mother and no child exposure — the drama is documentary (who writes it first), which is the scenario's thesis and is stated as such in its guide. The batch's only full win, labeled full in its own debrief.
**Deliberately not used:** no statement that closure is an entitlement on a completed record — the continued-supervision ending exists precisely to show the court's discretion surviving a perfect file when the ask shrinks.

## Scenario 96 — "Thirty Days After the Decree" (Complex, 39 nodes, 4 endings)

**Family:** Beatrix Lindqvist; her son Alban (7). A termination decree has been entered; her mother Greta keeps Alban's second bedroom. The foster parents' adoption petition is being prepared in parallel.
**Documented patterns drawn on:** termination-appeal practice as the project's appellate sources document it — preservation at trial (Scenario 24's discipline at maximum stakes), the certified record's completeness *and findability* (the misfiled-exhibit beat is a composite of ordinary record-assembly error), counsel's blown deadline (Scenario 88's clock, at professional scale, in the quashed branch), and the non-suspensive character of a pending appeal against a parallel adoption track.
**Law in the data:** Pa.R.A.P. 903(a) (newly verified); clear-and-convincing burden (verified set); § 3130.44(d). The two-question appellate frame (grounds / needs and welfare) is stated as counsel's framing without a § 2511 citation — deliberately withheld, as in prior batches: this project has never verified § 2511's text and does not teach termination grounds.
**Alterations:** composite in full; the decree's two weaknesses (an unexaminable summary report under one finding; a finding refuted by the agency's own admitted referral-log exhibit under another) are constructed so that both are *record* defects — the scenario can therefore be won on paper without the game predicting any real court's weighing of actual evidence.
**Deliberately not used:** no stay/supersedeas mechanics; no professional-liability mapping for the blown deadline (the ending states that remedies exist and are fact-specific, and stops — the boundary of general information); no depiction of the retrial.
