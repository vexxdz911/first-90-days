# STRATEGY GUIDE — Scenario 16: "The Second County" (Hard)

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation. This guide describes strategy inside the scenario only. It is general information about how the simulated system works — not legal advice for any real case.*

**Path check:** The route below was verified by walking `scenario-16.json` programmatically — every choice named here exists at the named node, and the sequence reaches the named ending. End cash on this route: $155 of $230.

## 1. The win

**"Ending — The File Arrives With Her."** The humblest win in the batch, and the guide will keep it humble: nothing is won except *continuity*. Your eight months still count as eight months — in both counties, in writing. Bexley credits your services from your own copies on day twenty-three instead of re-baselining you at month nine; when the official box arrives on day thirty-four missing two certificates and a month of visit logs, the gaps are already covered; at the Alder review (Monday, September 14, 2026 — start + 42 days, machine-checked) the transfer month is documented as a relocation, not a disappearance. Ivy's case proceeds at its true age. That is all, and it is the whole game.

## 2. The route

1. **The Week Before the Truck** — Choose *"Request the complete record now — before the move, while Alder still has you on its desk."* (cash −$12) Changes: sets **recordsRequested** and creates the binder — the only complete set of your records that will exist on Earth for the next month. Timing is the entire move: the request is easy in Alder this week and effectively impossible from Bexley next month.
2. **County Line** — Notify both counties of the move in writing. (cash −$4) Changes: your relocation is documented in both directions from day one; no one can later describe the move as a disappearance.
3. **Nothing Yet** — Choose *"Spend the fare and keep every visit."* (cash −$33 across the weeks) Changes: the visit log — kept in Alder, in a file in transit — never acquires the "missed during transfer" line that summaries love to rewrite as disengagement.
4. **A Room With a Lease Line** — Put the Bexley room on hold, documentation first. (cash −$20) Changes: adds the housing hidden item; makes the second pressure (Marisol's sixty-day guest clock) visible to the case instead of letting it ambush you at day sixty.
5. **The Border, in Practice** — Choose *"Lay the binder on the intake desk."* Changes: Bexley's file is *born* containing eight months of your life; services are verified and credited provisionally pending the official file. This node is the downstream beat the binder visibly changes — contrast "We Start When It Arrives," where Bexley schedules duplicate assessments and a fresh baseline.
6. **The File Arrives With Her** — Get the intake receipt. (cash −$3) Changes: adds the receipt exhibit proving *when* Bexley received your copies — the fact that protects the credit if the box later arrives incomplete.
7. **A Month of Receipts** — Index the transfer month in date order. Changes: the month the county files describe as blank becomes the most densely documented month of your case.
8. **The Clerk's Question** → **The Box** → **The Differences, Flagged** — Send the complete set through your lawyer to *both* counties before the hearing; reconcile box against binder and flag each gap with its covering document. Changes: sets **reconciliationFiled**; the one-page memo becomes the hearing.
9. **The Review — Two Counties on the Phone** — Choose *"Let the complete set answer."* Reaches **Ending — The File Arrives With Her**.

## 3. Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

- **The record right is portable; the file is not.** Under 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d), the case record "shall be released upon request" to its subject. Exercised *before* the move, it converts the agency's archive into your binder — the one artifact that crosses a county line at highway speed, ahead of interoffice process. Exercised never, your eight months exist only in other people's cabinets, in another county.
- **Clocks run on the child's life, not the file's itinerary.** The review date does not move because a box is in a courier's truck, and the case's timeline under the ASFA framework (42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E) — the 15-of-22-months accounting) keeps counting Ivy's months in care during the transfer. A month of undocumented services is a month that accounting cannot see; re-baselined services read as a parent earlier in her plan than she is. Continuity *is* the legal substance here — there is no other prize at this review.
- **Counsel coordinates what counties don't** (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337). The reconciliation memo — each gap in the transferred file paired with its covering document, sent to both counties before the hearing — is lawyer work in its purest form in this series: not argument, but accounting, delivered early.

## 4. The traps

- **Trusting the transfer** (*The Week Before the Truck*, second choice). "In process" is true and useless. You arrive in Bexley with a phone number and a story, Bexley's file opens from a stranger's summary of your eight months, and duplicate assessments follow: the on-ramp to **"Ending — Starting From Zero"** (recoverable only partway, via begging records loose, to "Half a Restart").
- **Skipping the intake receipt** — handing over copies without a dated, itemized receipt. If the box later arrives incomplete, there is no proof of what Bexley already had, or when; your credit for the transfer month rests on a memory.
- **Trusting the box at the review** (*The Box*, second choice, then systems-answer at *The Review*). The box is official *and* incomplete — it ends a month ago and lacks the loose items. Letting two counties' procedures explain each other to the judge produces a description of what happened to you, offered as an account of you.
- **Missing a visit without writing** (*Nothing Yet*, second choice). The log line appears either way; without the dated explanation to both counties, it is available to be summarized as "disengaged following relocation" — a sentence wrong about everything except the bus fare. (Writing late, as this path allows, is the partial repair — the ladder has many rungs, but the fare-keeping route never needs it.)

## 5. What no strategy controls

The box might arrive complete and early for another family. Marisol's sixty days might run out for yours and force a second move mid-case. A Bexley intake worker less willing to copy a parent's binder could insist on waiting for the official file — some do, within their rights and procedures. Couriers, clerks, cousins, and landlords all hold pieces of this outcome; the cousin's spare room holds more than most. In the published cases behind this scenario, families' cases crossed county lines and arrived thinned and re-baselined, read by new workers from partial files. What the route controls is the one participant who crosses the border at fifty-five miles an hour with every incentive to arrive complete: you, carrying the file like it's the case — because for a month, it is.
