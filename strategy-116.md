# Strategy Guide — Scenario 116: "The Summary Line"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the case, and every choice below are fictional. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "Ending — Corrected, Entry by Entry."**

State it honestly: this is the capstone, and it is a **win**, labeled as one — with the reason stated, as this series requires. At the permanency review, the summary line "unresolved financial instability" is corrected entry by entry on the record and struck, replaced by the three entries in their true shapes; on the corrected file the court orders Leona's return home on a short transition schedule. The guide can say the record earned it, and here is why that sentence is permitted: every other element of the case — treatment completed, visits kept, work steady — was **already documented as complete** before the phrase appeared, and the *only* obstacle to the case's existing direction was three money entries that the file's own paper refuted. The win is not that a mother argued well. It is that an audit, built entirely from the system's own documents, was filed in front of the room that owned the phrase, at a review taken **before** the fifteen-month date — with a documented-exception packet already on file as backstop, unneeded. Nothing less complete earns this ending, and the other endings show the prices of each incompleteness.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **The record is obtainable on request** — 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d) ("shall be released upon request"). The entire audit is that right exercised with named pages: the plan, the ledger, the posting record, the correspondence. Each entry is corrected *from the file's own documents* — which is why the room corrects them itself, out loud, instead of weighing a mother's word against a summary.
- **The review's determinations are made on the record before the court** — at a permanency review the court makes the determinations the law lists for reviews (42 Pa.C.S. § 6351(f), including the placement-duration determination, § 6351(f)(9)'s territory). Counsel's move is procedural and exact: correct each entry from its documents *before* the determinations are made, so the determinations rest on a truthful file. A record whose three load-bearing entries are corrected cannot carry the phrase built from them.
- **The ASFA backstop, in its disciplined form.** At 15 of the most recent 22 months, the agency is generally to file a termination petition **unless an exception is documented**: (i) the child is being cared for by a relative, (ii) a documented compelling reason that filing would not serve the child, or (iii) the agency has not provided services it identified as necessary for the child's safe return (42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(E)). The exception is **record-dependent** — it exists as an argument only to the extent the file documents it — and it speaks to the **filing decision** only. Here it was filed and never needed, because the correction at the earlier review removed the phrase the filing decision would have rested on. Had a petition been filed anyway, the agency would still have to prove its grounds by **clear and convincing evidence** at a hearing; no ending in this scenario depicts or decides such a hearing.
- **Counsel** throughout is the parent's right (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337) — used here for its least dramatic function: an audit, assembled into filings, before dated decisions are made from the file.

## (4) The traps

1. **Leaving any one tab unassembled** (no **entry1Doc**, **entry2Doc**, or **entry3Doc**). The review node carries a separate gated choice for each single-missing-entry combination, and all three lead to the same place: **Ending — The Phrase Survives on One Entry**. Two entries are corrected on the record; the phrase re-forms around the survivor, shorter and weaker and still the file's description of her money. Partial paper partially works, in exact proportion — this scenario is the batch's proof of that sentence.
2. **Auditing from the folder alone** (no **recordsRequested**). Her folder proves her life; the summary was not built from it, and the packet choice at the assembly node is gated on the record request — it never appears. What remains is the oral raise and waiting, both of which end at **Ending — The Phrase Stands Whole**.
3. **Raising the audit orally** (sets **oralRaised**). Three true stories, clearly told, filed in a transcript nobody summarizes — against a phrase sitting in a summary everybody reads. The exhibits stay in a binder in counsel's office.
4. **Taking the later review slot.** The calendar trap, priced in full by **Ending — The Date Strikes First**: the agency files on December 1 on the file as the date found it, and the audit debuts in a harder proceeding as an exhibit about grounds, instead of correcting a summary at a review. Reviews can be rescheduled. Filing dates are not reviews.

## (5) What no strategy controls

The new supervisor's reading of the same packets — a colder reader corrects two entries and keeps the phrase on the third, and the route's paper then travels into the proceeding of ending two or four. The summary writer who read one column; the worker who wrote a pause note and never carried it across her own file; the queue's speed; the court's calendar and its two polite slots; and luck, in the specific form of which offices keep their envelopes. The route controls only this: every entry was audited from documents, the audit was filed before the room that owned the phrase, the backstop was filed before the date that owned the calendar, and the review was taken while the file could still change what the date would do. Outcomes in this system turn on workers, supervisors, judges, and luck as much as on any parent's choices — and never on a parent's worth.
