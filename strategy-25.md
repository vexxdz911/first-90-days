# Strategy Guide — Scenario 25: "The Expert Split"

*Game documentation for a fictional simulation in "The First 90 Days" (Tactic Watch). The family, the injury, the experts, and every choice below are fictional composites drawn from published opinions' mechanics, not from any real case. This guide is general information about how the scenario is built — it is not legal advice for any real case, and the medicine in the scenario is deliberately plain and general.*

## (1) The win

**Best ending: "Not Sustained — Home."**

This one can be stated plainly: it is a real win. The agency's petition is dismissed at adjudication because, on the complete record, its proof does not reach clear and convincing evidence, and Wren comes home. Two honest qualifications, stated up front rather than in fine print: (a) the win is a ruling about *the record and the burden*, not a finding that nothing happened or a certificate of perfect parenting — the court's own reasoning credits the agency's expert as careful and finds her proof, against a complete file, insufficient; and (b) the indicated-report track is a *separate* proceeding that this hearing does not decide. The route below also files that track in time, so the win doesn't quietly rot in a drawer — but its outcome is decided elsewhere, under a different burden, and this guide does not promise it.

## (2) The route

Verified by walking the scenario graph from the start node (see the verification note at the end). Key decision points, in order — node title, winning choice, and what it changes:

1. **Two Versions of the Weekend** — *Ask for copies of everything — imaging, notes, all of it — before you leave the hospital.* Cash −15 (you finish the route with $90 of the $185 start). Sets **recordsRequested** on day 0 — the earliest possible start to the chase.
2. **Questions at the Kitchen Table** — *Answer fully, and start your own dated notebook as soon as they leave.* Sets **ownNotes**. The raw material the timeline will later be built from, instead of from midnight memory.
3. **The First Visit** — *Write the visit into the notebook the same day.* Sets **visitNotes**. Same-day notes are the closest thing to a second witness a parent can honestly manufacture.
4. **Your Own Doctor, Carefully Neutral** — *Request the pediatrician's complete records in writing, today.* Cash −10. Sets **pedsRecords** (and **recordsRequested**). Pre-case paper — a scheduled, attended history of care — requested before events can edit anyone's account of it.
5. **The Records Chase Begins** — *Work the checklist in order: complete imaging set first, signed releases everywhere.* Cash −15. Sets **imagingFirst**. Order is the game here: the third opinion's value is decided by what is sent first and fastest.
6. **Portals, Fax Numbers, Business Days** — *Keep the chase log: every request, date, name, promise.* Sets **chaseLog**. If a page is missing at the hearing, the log converts "we asked" from a claim into a fact.
7. **Building the Weekend** — *Build the timeline day by day, hour by hour, with every timestamp printed and attached.* Cash −10. Sets **timelineBuilt** — a load-bearing flag. This replaces the chart's bare phrase "inconsistent accounts" with documents: texts, schedules, receipts, showing two frightened people disagreeing by one evening, not a shifting story.
8. **The Second Letter** — *Calendar the indicated report's deadline in ink.* Sets **indicatedTracked**. The separate track, with its own clock, is now on its own checklist instead of in a drawer.
9. **The Disc** — *Sign the correct release, pay the copy fee, and leave with the complete imaging set in hand.* Cash −20. Sets **completeImaging** — the scenario's central flag. Without the full set (including follow-up views), every later expert is re-reading someone else's summary.
10. **Growth Records and Grandparents** — *Assemble the full packet now.* Cash −10. Sets **packetAssembled**: imaging, growth records, labs, timeline, statements, tabbed in one binder. Then, at **The Grandparents' Two Hours**, file their rewritten statement — one page, dated, times only — setting **gpStatement** and closing the timeline's only gap.
11. **The Third Opinion** — *Send the complete packet and confirm, in writing, that the specialist has everything and a hearing date to work toward.* Sets **thirdOpinionSent**. Then, at **The Specialist's Office Calls Back**, send the one missing growth-chart page by confirmed delivery (cash −5, sets **chartSent**) — the last moment a missing page is an errand instead of an absence.
12. **The Other Track's Paperwork** — *File the written expunction request now, well inside the deadline, and request the agency's investigation evidence.* Cash −10. Sets **indicatedAppealFiled**. This keeps the split ending ("Split — Dependent, But the Report Is Expunged") reachable as a fallback even if the hearing goes the other way.
13. **The Week Before the Hearing** — *Go forward on the record as built.* This is a route choice, not a flag: asking for a continuance routes to "The Continuance That Ran Out," where the court grants days, the specialist needs weeks, and the hearing proceeds on the two-expert record anyway.
14. **The Court Weighs the Paper** — *The record was complete — timeline, imaging, growth records, and a third opinion the court could weigh.* This choice is flag-gated: it requires **timelineBuilt** + **completeImaging** + **thirdOpinionSent**. It leads to **Not Sustained — Home**.

Notice what the route never does: it never picks a "right" doctor in a dialogue, never charms a caseworker, never performs sincerity for the court. Every winning move is a trip to a counter, a signature, a stamp, or a printed timestamp. That is the scenario's thesis, and the route is just the thesis with page numbers.

## (3) Why this wins — the law as it is supposed to work

Only principles from the scenario's verified set are stated here:

- **Speed and counsel at the front end.** After the emergency removal, the shelter care hearing must be held not later than 72 hours after placement (42 Pa.C.S. § 6332; 23 Pa.C.S. § 6315(d)) — in this scenario, the deadline is computed from the removal date and met — and the parent has the right to counsel and to appointed counsel (42 Pa.C.S. § 6337). The route's early record-building happens inside that structure, with counsel directing it.
- **Records are released on request** — 55 Pa. Code § 3130.44(d). Steps 1, 4, and 5 are that right exercised provider by provider, in writing, with a log. The completeness the hearing later weighs exists only because the requests did.
- **Dependency must be proven by clear and convincing evidence.** The hearing is not a vote between two doctors' personalities; it is a burden, applied to a record. A complete record — the full imaging set both sides' experts can actually read, the growth history, the labs, a timeline built from contemporaneous documents — is what lets a court separate a *diagnosis* (a statement about an injury) from an *accusation* (a statement about a person) and ask whether the proof walks, clearly and convincingly, from one to the other.
- **The indicated report is a separate track with its own clock and its own burden.** The written expunction request must be made within 90 days of notice (23 Pa.C.S. § 6341(a)(2)) — the scenario computes that deadline from the fictional notice date — and at the expunction hearing the *agency* must prove its report by substantial evidence (23 Pa.C.S. § 6341(c.2)(5)), with the appellant entitled to the investigation evidence the agency relied on (23 Pa.C.S. § 6341(c.2)(4)). Filing on time (step 12) is what keeps that second, differently-burdened decision available at all.

## (4) The traps

1. **Taking the hospital's written report as "good enough" at The Disc.** Cost: no **completeImaging**. The third opinion becomes a review of a summary; the gated winning choice at the decision node closes; the court ends up weighing one side's full read against your side's book report. This is the most expensive $20 saving in the scenario.
2. **Writing the timeline "from memory — the gist is the same" at Building the Weekend.** Cost: no **timelineBuilt**. The chart's three words — "inconsistent accounts" — remain the only version with a letterhead, both the winning ending *and* the split ending close (both are gated on the timeline), and cross-examination gets to eat the gist at leisure.
3. **Waiting to file the expunction request until "after the dependency outcome."** Cost: the second track. The 90-day clock does not pause for the first track's drama; miss it and the indicated entry — with its quiet effects on childcare work and clearances — outlives the hearing by years, whatever the hearing decides.
4. **Asking for a continuance at The Week Before the Hearing** to wait for a third opinion that hasn't landed. Cost: the hearing itself. The court grants days, the specialist works in weeks, and the case is decided on the record that existed — while the eventual report arrives afterward as the binder's thickest, saddest tab. Time does not assemble records; people at counters do, weeks earlier.

## (5) What no strategy controls

Which expert the judge credits — both are qualified, both are sincere, and credibility calls are the trial court's to make. The fracture itself, and the first doctor's reading of it. The specialist's queue, the imaging center's fax machine, the hearing officer on the other track reaching a different answer on overlapping facts. And luck, in its ordinary costume: which office answers the phone, which envelope arrives on which day. The route controls exactly one thing — the completeness and timeliness of the paper in the room when the weighing happens. In this scenario, as the design intends, that turns out to be enough to matter without ever being a guarantee. An adjudication measures a record and a risk. It does not measure a parent's worth, and no strategy in this guide should be read as claiming otherwise.
